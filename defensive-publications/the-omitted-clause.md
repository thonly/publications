## Abstract

This study reads the collapse narrative of the *Cakkavatti-Sīhanāda Sutta* (Dīgha Nikāya 26) as a causal model of societal breakdown under economic deprivation, and applies it to technological unemployment from artificial intelligence. In the text a ruler arranges protection for his realm but omits one duty, giving property to those without means; poverty spreads and theft follows; the ruler first gives property to men who steal and then has the next one executed; each response is followed by escalation, and the sequence ends in seven days of mutual killing. The study does three things with the model. It calibrates it against Cambodia: rural indebtedness and the Samlaut rising of 1967 run its middle stages, while the 1970 coup, civil war and United States bombing are accelerants it does not contain, so the omission reads as an enabling condition rather than a sole cause; the Khmer Rouge, which promised provision and delivered it by violent seizure, reads as the clause's counterfeit. It projects the model onto AI-driven economic displacement (loss of work, then income, then property, then home) and reads the text's two failed responses as two policy reflexes: a transfer that rewards disruption after the fact, and criminalization of the displaced. It argues that a social minimum installed before displacement works as insurance, cheap if displacement never comes and decisive if it does, and that the text, told inside an exhortation, reads as prophecy told for prevention. A further section answers the objection that Cambodia's role in this decade as a host of industrial online fraud refutes the reading, treating it as a low-capacity jurisdiction rented by foreign capital, never as a national character. The study discloses no mechanism; its findings are enumerated with what would break each.

## Findings disclosed

The paper discloses no mechanism. Its findings are listed here, each with what would break it; the sections named carry the argument. No novelty census has been run on this paper and none of these findings asserts priority; where a finding has a nearest prior instance, the section named cites it.

1. **DN 26's collapse narrative reads as a causal model with three properties: the trigger is an omission (the welfare clause, dropped while the duty of guard and protection is adopted); propagation runs through locally reasonable responses that worsen the system; and the text names two responses that skip provision — property given to men who have stolen, then execution of the next one — each followed by escalation** (§2; DN 26 §91–§94). *Breaks if:* the text is shown to locate the decline's trigger elsewhere than the omission, or to present the gift to the thief as the clause fulfilled.
2. **The clause and the failed subsidy differ in timing and addressee, not in what is handed over.** Both give property; the clause gives it to those without before anyone takes, the subsidy to the man who took, because he took (§2, §4.2). *Breaks if:* the text is shown to treat property given after the theft, to the taker, as fulfilment of the duty.
3. **Cambodia's catastrophe fits the model as an enabling condition, not a sole cause.** Rural indebtedness, land pressure and the Samlaut rising of 1967 (compulsory rice collection, revolt, military suppression, radicalization) run the model's middle stages; the 1970 coup and the prince's alliance with the insurgency, the civil war, and United States bombing are accelerants the model does not contain (§3.1–§3.2). *Breaks if:* the historiography shows the insurgency's rural growth arising without economic grievance, or the Samlaut sequence running in another order.
4. **The Khmer Rouge is the clause's counterfeit: it campaigned on provision and delivered it by violent seizure and forced collectivization, and the stage the text calls *migasaññā* arrived inside the regime; provision by gift and provision by violent seizure are opposite operations** (§3.3). The finding does not reach lawful, compensated reform or tax-funded provision (§7.5). *Breaks if:* a regime is shown to have delivered provision by extra-legal, violent seizure at national scale and to have been followed by stability rather than escalating violence.
5. **Read forward, the cascade runs through loss of work, income and property to loss of home, and the text's two failed responses correspond to two policy reflexes under displacement pressure: a transfer that answers disruption after it occurs, and criminalization of the displaced** (§4.1–§4.2). *Breaks if:* displacement at scale arrives and runs a different sequence, or polities meet it with neither reflex.
6. **A social minimum installed before displacement is insurance-shaped: its case does not depend on the displacement forecast** (§4.3, §5). *Breaks if:* the floor's cost in the scenario without displacement is shown to be large relative to the surplus it draws on, or a floor installed early is shown not to interrupt the later stages where displacement does arrive.
7. **DN 26 is told inside an exhortation to the monks (DN 26 §80, §110), which supports reading its decline as a warning; the commentary supports a narrower form, in which the *form* of an age's intermediate destruction follows the root that predominates, the sword where hatred prevails** (§6, §7.3). *Breaks if:* the text or its commentary is shown to present the sword-interval as fixed regardless of conduct.
8. **Cambodia's place in this decade's online-fraud industry is the cascade's tail, not a refutation of §6: the interval destroyed the apparatus that names theft as theft, the floor came decades later, and a low-capacity jurisdiction can be rented by foreign capital — a claim about a jurisdiction, never a national character** (§6.1). *Breaks if:* the compounds are shown to have been staffed and financed mainly from within Cambodia, or enforcement capacity is shown to have been high while the industry grew.
9. **The conditions of an industrial fraud economy and of a cross-border gratitude ledger are the same three — a thin trust apparatus, a diaspora with cross-border ties, and instant mobile payment rails with weak recourse — and the two are sorted by intention; that the second is built from inside the country is a selection effect, not a metaphysics** (§6.1, §7.2a). *Breaks if:* one of the three conditions is shown to be absent from either economy, or the coincidence is shown to need an explanation beyond shared conditions and selection.

## 1 · Introduction — the vision and the two questions

This paper originated in a single sitting. The founder, reading the parent paper in its published form, returned with four sentences: *Was poverty the exact hinge that cascaded into the sword-interval in Cambodia? The greatest failure mode of AI and robots could be poverty on a global scale — loss of job, loss of income, loss of property. Without a dignity floor, the world can spiral into another sword-interval, on a macrocosmic scale. Cambodia's historical contribution is the microcosmic sword-interval: a macrocosmic sword-interval is not necessary for Metteyya to appear.*

Those four sentences contain a historical question, a risk claim, a design consequence, and a thesis about meaning. The paper takes them in order: §2 compresses the cascade grammar from the parent paper; §3 answers the historical question with the calibration it deserves; §4 defends the risk claim in the vocabulary of the contemporary literature and adds what the sutta contributes to it; §5 states the design consequence — the dignity floor as insurance — and its working instantiation; §6 states the vaccine thesis; §7 calibrates honestly; §8–§10 close with lineage, conclusion, and citations.

## 2 · The cascade grammar, compressed

DN 26's collapse narrative, reduced to its causal skeleton (full treatment in the parent paper, §2 and §5):

```
        guard and protection adopted — MINUS the welfare clause
        ("no property to those without")
                        │
                   POVERTY spreads
                        │
           a man takes what is not given
                        │
        ┌───────────────┴────────────────┐
   CORRECTION 1                     CORRECTION 2
   the perverse subsidy             the punitive crackdown
   (give the taker property —       (execute the next taker —
   teach that taking pays)          answer need with force)
        └───────────────┬────────────────┘
                 both skip the clause;
                 both accelerate the fall
                        │
          violence → killing → lying → …
                        │
              migasaññā: persons
              perceived as prey
                        │
         SATTHANTARAKAPPA — the sword-interval
                        │
          the survivors' vow: "let us do good"
                        │
              recovery, generation
              upon generation
```

Three properties matter for everything that follows. The trigger is an *omission*, not an attack: the king, advised of the duty, arranges righteous guard and protection but gives no property to those without (*no ca kho adhanānaṃ dhanamanuppadāsi*, DN 26 §91). The propagation is *incentive-mediated* — each stage is a locally reasonable response that worsens the system. And the text supplies not one but **two failed corrections**. The king first gives a man who has stolen property to live on, and then a second; when others steal in order to be given the same, he has the next one executed, after which people arm themselves, raid villages and towns, and kill those they rob (DN 26 §91–§93). Between them the two corrections name the responses the text considers that skip structural provision: pay off the symptom, or punish it. On this reading the sutta's position is that there was one correct move, and its timing is the whole point — the welfare clause (clause 2 in the parent paper's mapping), given to those without *before* the first theft. The clause and the failed subsidy differ in timing and addressee, not in what is handed over: both give property, the clause to those without before anyone takes, the subsidy to the man who took, because he took.

## 3 · Backward: was poverty the hinge in Cambodia?

### 3.1 · What the history supports

The Khmer Rouge recruited in a countryside under strain. The historiography documents deepening peasant indebtedness to moneylenders through the 1960s, land pressure in the north-west, and a widening rural–urban divide; Kiernan's history of the movement's rise traces how it built its rural networks on those grievances and offered the revolution as the reversal of a long dispossession. And one episode runs the sutta's middle beats in miniature, eight years before the fall: **Samlaut, 1967** — compulsory state rice collection at fixed low prices (extraction from those already without), peasant revolt (the theft-stage answered in kind), brutal military suppression (the punitive correction), and a wave of radicalization flowing from the crackdown, the opening of what Kiernan's history treats as the first civil war (1967–70). The cascade's grammar — omission, desperation, taking, punishment, escalation — is legible in the record.

### 3.2 · What the history adds that the sutta does not model

Honesty requires the accelerants. The Cambodian catastrophe cannot be narrated from internal causes alone. The March 1970 coup deposed Sihanouk, who then lent his name to a front whose fighting force was the Khmer Rouge, so that the peasantry's attachment to the monarchy became one of the movement's recruiting assets. The civil war that followed was embedded in a regional war. And the United States bombed the countryside: Owen and Kiernan's 2006 estimate of about 2.76 million tons of ordnance rested on a database later found to contain errors, and their 2010 article puts the tonnage at no less than about half a million tons and possibly far more, citing a CIA field report of May 1973 that recorded the insurgents using airstrike damage in their recruiting propaganda. These supplied wind the sutta's closed-system model does not contain. The honest verdict on the founder's question is therefore: **enabling condition, not mono-cause.** The omitted clause opened the door; foreign fire and war drove the storm through it. A constitution's failure model does not need to be the whole causal story to be the load-bearing one — the sutta names the condition without which the accelerants would have found no tinder.

### 3.3 · The counterfeit clause

The sharpest finding is grammatical, and it is the one this institution's design grammar is built to see. The Khmer Rouge did not omit the welfare clause rhetorically — it *campaigned on it*. Rice for all; the peasantry raised up; the dispossession reversed. And it delivered the clause's counterfeit: provision **by taking** — expropriation, forced collectivization, enslavement, class hatred as the theft-stage elevated to a state program. What followed is the historical record's own commentary on the difference: the regime that came to power on the omitted clause produced, inside four years, the closest lived approach to the sutta's sword-interval — including the stage the text renders as *migasaññā*, the perception of persons as prey (the text's own simile is a hunter who sees a deer, DN 26 §103), which survivor testimony describes without needing the Pāli. **Provision-by-gift and provision-by-taking are opposite operations with opposite ends.** The clause cannot be satisfied by its parody; a floor built from violent seizure is a trapdoor. This distinction — gift and violent seizure as the two poles of provision, with lawful taxation and compensated reform lying between them and outside this finding (§7.5) — sits beside the gift/exchange boundary of this corpus's design grammar (after Mauss) and is the starker of the two: exchange is the gift's neighbour, seizure its opposite. It is why the institution this corpus documents builds its floor from circulation and gift rather than from taking of any kind.

## 4 · Forward: the macrocosmic instance

### 4.1 · The scenario

The largest plausible instance of the omitted clause now on humanity's horizon, on this paper's reading, is the economic displacement scenario of the AI transition. Its skeleton is the founder's sentence: **loss of job → loss of income → loss of property** — to which this paper adds the terminal term the Cambodian record supplies: **→ loss of home.** The scenario has a literature. Economists have argued over machinery and employment at least since Ricardo added his chapter "On Machinery" in 1821, and Keynes gave the phrase *technological unemployment* its currency in 1930; the automation-wave studies of the 2010s (Frey and Osborne; Autor; Brynjolfsson and McAfee) mapped task-level exposure; and the alignment community has begun to formalize the systemic version — the *gradual disempowerment* analysis of Kulveit and colleagues, in which AI's replacement of human labour and cognition erodes human influence over the economy, culture and the state together, each erosion reinforcing the others, without any single dramatic event — as a civilizational risk class distinct from takeover. The cascade is the same shape at every scale: those without provision take, or rage, or are criminalized; polities respond with the two failed corrections; mutual perception degrades — the *migasaññā* stage has a modern name, dehumanizing polarization — and the spiral tightens.

### 4.2 · What the sutta adds to the scenario literature

The gradual-disempowerment literature describes the slope. DN 26 adds three things to it. **The hinge**: a precise claim about *which* omission initiates the slide — not oversight failure, not value misspecification in the machines, but the welfare clause omitted in the polity. **The two failed corrections, as a taxonomy**: confronted with displacement, modern polities reach for the perverse subsidy or the punitive crackdown. The text's subsidy is defined by its timing and its addressee — property given to the man who took, after and because he took — and its modern analogue is the transfer that arrives only once disruption has occurred, in answer to it, teaching that disruption pays (and, in this corpus's further reading, disbursed without dignity or function); the crackdown's analogue is criminalizing the displaced — vagrancy law, encampment sweeps, workfare coercion. Where a given modern transfer falls is a question of its design, which this paper does not adjudicate for any particular programme; the line it draws is the text's, between provision given before the taking to those without and provision given after it to the taker. The sutta presents both responses, in order, as accelerants, because both *skip the clause*, which specifies provision to those without before the first theft. **The timing claim**: the clause is preventive, not remedial. Installed before the cascade's first stage, it is cheap. Given after the first theft, and to the thief, the same property teaches theft: in the text the people, hearing that the king gives property to those who steal, resolve to steal too, and the king himself foresees that giving to whoever steals will make theft grow (DN 26 §92).

### 4.3 · Why this wave may differ — stated with its counter-argument

The standard economist's objection is history: every automation wave since the loom has destroyed tasks and created more, and the lump-of-labor fallacy is a fallacy. The honest reply is that the objection may hold again — and that this wave has properties the previous ones did not: *generality* (prior waves automated task categories; a general substitute for cognition and, with robotics, for dexterity, addresses the whole task distribution at once), *speed* (diffusion in years, not generations — faster than retraining, faster than politics), and *capital concentration* (the returns to the automating asset may accrue to its owners more steeply than in earlier waves). This paper does not adjudicate the forecast. It does not need to: §5's argument is insurance-shaped, and insurance does not require certainty about the fire.

Insurance logic, not forecast logic.

## 6 · The vaccine thesis

The founder's fourth sentence is the paper's deepest claim, and the paper argues that it is doctrinally sound, in the form §7.3 calibrates: **a macrocosmic sword-interval is not necessary.**

In DN 26, Metteyya arises only after the full sequence — omission, cascade, sword, vow, recovery (DN 26 §107). Read fatalistically, the text schedules the catastrophe. But the fatalistic reading mistakes the genre. The Buddha tells DN 26 to the monks inside an exhortation: it opens and closes with *keep to your own pasture, your ancestral domain* (*gocare, bhikkhave, caratha sake pettike visaye*, DN 26 §80, §110). At the opening the pasture is the four establishments of mindfulness, where Māra finds no foothold; at the close, the monk who keeps to it grows in lifespan, beauty, happiness, wealth and strength — the very goods whose decline the king's story narrates. This paper reads the text's own pragmatics as **prophecy-as-prevention**. The middle act is narrated so that it need not be lived. A constitution that dramatizes its own violation is not commanding the violation; it is inoculating against it.

And an inoculation requires antigen — a real instance, encountered safely. That is what the microcosm is. Cambodia ran the complete trace — the omission's grammar, the counterfeit clause, the cascade, the sword-interval, the survivors' vow, and the recovery from carriers along channels no fire could reach — at national scale, within living memory, documented end to end. The species does not need to run the experiment again at planetary scale, for the same reason a body with antibodies does not need the disease: **the tuition has been paid, once, in full, by one small country.** What remains is for the receipt to be read. That is Cambodia's historical contribution in the founder's framing, and this paper is written so that the framing exists in the literature: not the wound as identity, but the wound as *dāna to the species* — the complete empirical warning, given at the highest price a culture can pay, that the omitted clause is real, that its counterfeit is worse, and that the correct move is the clause itself, kept early, funded by gift.

Under the parent paper's held-both-ways reading of the horizon — Metteyya as literal future Buddha, or as the flowering of universal loving-kindness — the vaccine thesis lands identically: the collapse was never the requirement. **The restoration is.** The conditions can be built without the fall; the sutta was told precisely so that they would be.

### 6.1 · The objection: if the vow held, what is the scam economy?

A reader who knows the region will have an objection ready, and it is the strongest this paper has found against §6. The vaccine thesis asks the species to read Cambodia's history as a completed warning — tuition paid, receipt available. But in this decade the country that paid it has been one of the principal hosts of industrialised online fraud: forced-labour compounds running investment and romance scams at global scale, with victims worldwide. **If the vow held, what is that?** And if it did not hold, the thesis loses its instance.

The answer this section defends is that the objection mistakes which thing the vow was about, and that the scam economy is not a refutation of the cascade grammar but **a long-running confirmation of it.**

**The cascade's tail outlasts the sword-interval.** §3 read the Cambodian catastrophe as omission → desperation → taking → punishment → escalation. What a sword-interval destroys is not only lives; it is the *apparatus by which a society names theft as theft* — courts, land records, professional cadres, the ordinary institutional memory that makes property legible and fraud prosecutable. The survivors' vow was *never again this*, and it held in the sense that matters here: the mass killing of 1975–79 did not return, though war continued until the movement's last forces surrendered at the end of the 1990s. **But a vow against the sword is not a floor.** §4.2's timing claim applies here at national scale and with unusual force. The clause is preventive, and Cambodia's came late: a national social protection strategy dates from 2011, a policy framework from 2016, and a national cash transfer to poor households from June 2020 — three to four decades after 1979, while the apparatus that names theft as theft was still thin. A country that rebuilds for decades without the floor, and with that apparatus thin, is in the state the grammar predicts will keep generating taking long after the violence has stopped.

**And it generated a form DN 26 has no term for.** The sutta models theft as *local and directed inward*: a man takes from his neighbour, the king responds, the spiral tightens inside one polity. What the last decade produced in the region is theft **industrialised, foreign-capitalised, and aimed outward** — an export industry whose victims are largely not Cambodian and whose operators are largely not Cambodian either. The canon has no term for the mechanism. Political economy has a near one, Palan's *commercialization of state sovereignty*, written for tax havens that sell legal shelter to capital from elsewhere; this paper states the form that fits this case: **a low-capacity jurisdiction is a jurisdiction that can be rented.** Where institutional capacity is thin, sovereignty itself becomes a leasable input — territory, legal shelter, and non-enforcement sold to capital that arrives from elsewhere and departs when the pressure rises. **The omitted clause does not only produce thieves at home; it produces a place where other people's thieves can stand.** That is the cascade's grammar completing itself through an aperture a text more than two thousand years old could not have modelled, and it is the extension of the sutta's model that this section adds.

**Two constraints on this analysis, both binding.**

**This is a claim about a jurisdiction and never about a national character.** The compounds have been largely staffed by foreign nationals, many of them trafficked — the United Nations human rights office's 2023 report cited credible estimates of about 100,000 people held in Cambodia in situations where they were forced to carry out online scams — and largely financed by foreign capital; by the government's own count, 58,266 foreign nationals were rescued and deported between July 2025 and 20 August 2026. One case shows the pattern. The chairman of the Prince Group, Chen Zhi, was born in Fujian and acquired Cambodian citizenship in 2014. On 14 October 2025 the U.S. Treasury, in coordination with the United Kingdom, sanctioned him and the Prince Group network, and the U.S. Justice Department unsealed an indictment and filed a civil forfeiture action against some 127,271 bitcoin, valued at about fifteen billion dollars, which it described as the largest forfeiture action in its history. His Cambodian citizenship was revoked by royal decree in December 2025; he was arrested on 6 January 2026 and extradited to China within days. **Nothing in the cascade grammar indicts a people, and any reading of this section that does has misread it at the first step.**

**The state is not inert, and the paper would be dishonest to imply it.** Cambodia's National Assembly passed the Law on Combating Technology-Based Fraud (also reported as the Law on Anti-Technology Fraud) unanimously on 30 March 2026; the Senate adopted it unanimously on 3 April, and it was promulgated by royal decree on 6 April. It is the country's first statute aimed specifically at online scams, and as reported it carries two to five years' imprisonment for individual offenders, five to ten for ringleaders, ten to twenty where an operation involves trafficking, confinement, torture or forced labour, and fifteen to thirty years or life where an operation causes a death. By the government's count, between July 2025 and 20 August 2026 it investigated 793 suspected sites and raided 624, detaining nearly 30,000 suspects of 39 nationalities; in late August 2026 it declared that no scam compounds remained in the country, a claim Amnesty International and independent anti-trafficking researchers publicly doubted. External pressure has reached the political tier: the U.S. Treasury sanctioned the senator and tycoon Ly Yong Phat and his conglomerate in September 2024, and in April 2026 another sitting senator, Kok An, together with twenty-eight affiliated individuals and entities. A state that revokes a magnate's citizenship by royal decree and extradites him is not a state that has declined to act. **What the record holds is two things at once: enforcement that is real, and a capture that reached the legislature and has been met in part by foreign sanctions — which is what a late floor and a thin apparatus predict, and what a rented jurisdiction looks like when the rent is finally contested.**

**Same soil, opposite polarity.** The founder's question that prompted this section was whether it is coincidence or balance that the institution this corpus documents is being built by a Cambodian, from the diaspora, at the same moment and in the same country as the fraud industry. The structural answer requires no cosmology. **The input list is identical for both.** A trust vacuum left by an unrestored institutional apparatus; a large diaspora with money, obligation, and cross-border ties; payment rails that leapfrogged straight to mobile and settle instantly with weak recourse. Those three conditions are the preconditions of an industrialised scam economy *and* the preconditions of a cross-border gratitude ledger. **One field, two harvests, sorted by intention.**

That sorting is not a new idea in this paper; it is §3.3's finding at a third scale. Provision-by-gift and provision-by-taking were shown there to be opposite operations on the same clause, at the scale of a regime. Here they are opposite operations on the same *conditions*, at the scale of an economy. In the tradition's own vocabulary the sharpest statement of it is this: **what has been built in that jurisdiction is wrong livelihood (*micchā-ājīva*) industrialised, in a country whose canon was rebuilt from what its survivors carried — and what this corpus documents is an institution that counts right livelihood (*sammā-ājīva*, a factor of the path in SN 56.11) among its stated aims, built from the same wreckage by descendants of the people who lived through it.** Dependent origination does not require that the two be related by anything more than sharing a field. It requires only that the same conditions, met by different intentions, yield different fruit.

**And the caution belongs on the question rather than the answer.** See §7.2a.

## 7 · Honest calibration

**7.1 · Scenario, not prediction.** §4 defends a risk class, not a forecast. The displacement scenario has real dampeners — welfare-state machinery, policy learning, the adaptive record of past automation waves — and real amplifiers — generality, speed, concentration, and autonomous force. This paper assigns no probability. Its §5 argument is constructed to be sound under uncertainty: insurance, not prophecy.

**7.2 · The mapping flatters, again.** As in the parent paper, the clause-to-history and clause-to-scenario mappings are interpretations performed by motivated readers. §3.2 is the discipline: where the history exceeds the sutta's model, the paper says so, and the verdict is enabling-condition, never mono-cause. Cambodia's catastrophe had authors and accelerants a canonical grammar cannot indict; nothing here simplifies that record.

**7.2a · The seduction fires on the question, not on the answer.** §6.1 was prompted by asking whether it is coincidence or balance that this institution is being built from the same country and moment as the fraud industry. The structural answer given there is defensible. **The question is not, and the asymmetry is worth naming.** Had Cambodia in 2026 been notable for anything else — a manufacturing boom, a monastic revival, a currency crisis — a reader in the founder's position would have found that meaningful too, and would have had a grammar ready to make it so. **Any state of the country would have read as significant to someone looking for significance.** What survives the deflation is smaller and still worth having: it is unsurprising, and mildly evidential, that a counterweight to an extraction industry should be assembled by people from inside the wound it was made in, because those are the people to whom the wound is legible and the obligation is personal. **That is a selection effect, not a metaphysics**, and this paper claims no more from the coincidence than that.

**7.3 · The vaccine thesis is meaning-making, honestly owned.** §6 is not an empirical claim; it is an interpretive act — the assignment of prospective meaning to retrospective suffering. The authors believe it is the *right* act: it is performed by a member of the survivor culture, in its own tradition's genre (prophecy-as-prevention), toward the explicit end that the suffering not recur anywhere. But a reader is owed the label: this is a thesis about what Cambodia's history should *mean* to the species, and meaning is argued, not measured. The commentary on DN 26 supports a narrower form of the doctrinal premise than §6 states. It glosses the sword-interval as a destruction of the world that comes in between, short of the aeon of dissolution, and names three kinds: by famine where greed predominates among beings, by disease where delusion does, by the sword where hatred does (*lobhussadāya pajāya dubbhikkhantarakappo hoti. Mohussadāya rogantarakappo. Dosussadāya satthantarakappo*; Sumaṅgalavilāsinī on DN 26 §104). A Vinaya subcommentary, the Sāratthadīpanī, pairs famine and disease with the other two roots the other way round, and agrees that hatred brings the sword. So the tradition makes the *form* of the fall follow the root that prevails; neither text says that an intermediate destruction of some kind can be avoided altogether. §6's stronger claim, that the fall itself is unnecessary, rests on the exhortation frame and is this paper's reading.

**7.4 · n = 1 and n = 0.** The microcosmic instance is one country; the macrocosmic scenario has not occurred; the instantiated floor is one small institution. Every load-bearing empirical quantity in this paper is a 1 or a 0. The papers exist, in part, so that the record of what was claimed — and when, and on what grounds — is fixed before the numbers arrive.

**7.5 · The counterfeit has lawful neighbours.** §3.3 sets provision by gift against provision by violent seizure, and does not reach the cases between them. The land reforms of post-war Japan (1946) and Taiwan (1953) compelled landlords to sell to the state at prices it fixed — Taiwan's paid in land bonds and in shares of state enterprises — and both are widely credited with rural stability rather than with a cascade; tax-funded social protection is likewise compelled, and lawful. This paper treats neither as the counterfeit and adjudicates neither. Its finding is confined to provision delivered by extra-legal, violent seizure, which is what the Khmer Rouge practised.

**7.6 · Cambodia is an instance, not the instance.** Other catastrophes have been read as land and income pressure ending in mass killing; André and Platteau's study of one Rwandan commune before 1994 is the case most often cited in those terms. This paper does not survey them. It claims Cambodia as a full instance of the sutta's trace, collapse and recovery both, inside the tradition that carries the text — not as the only one.

**7.7 · The living monarchy.** This paper inherits the parent paper's guard in full. The Kingdom of Cambodia has a reigning royal house; this paper's engagement with DN 26's kingship is textual and archetypal, and neither this paper nor this institution makes, implies, or will make any claim touching the constitutional monarchy, its standing, or its succession. The royal decrees named in §6.1 are cited as public acts of state.

## 9 · Conclusion

A text more than two thousand years old says that civilizations do not fall to their enemies first; they fall to an omission — the clause that provisions those without — and that once the fall begins, the two corrections polities reach for both make it worse, because both are substitutes for the clause rather than the clause. One country has run the full experiment, including the counterfeit of the clause, which proved worse than the omission. One transition now approaches in which the same omission could be committed at planetary scale, faster than politics adapts, by default rather than by decision. The design consequence fits in a sentence: **install the clause early, fund it by gift, attach it to dignity and function, and extend it to the last step of the fall — the gate, the meal, the bed, the warmth.** The canonical consequence also fits in one: on this paper's reading, the prophecy was told so that it would not happen. And the historical consequence is Cambodia's to give and the world's to receive: the sword-interval has already been survived once, by a people who came out of the forests and vowed to do good, and whose vow against the sword has held for nearly fifty years. §6.1 records what the vow alone did not do: a vow against the sword is not a floor, and the floor came late. The record shows the fallen raised; the prevention is left to the clause, kept early.
