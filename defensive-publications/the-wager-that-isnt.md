## Abstract

Charity sports tournaments — golf outings, boxing galas, celebrity matches — monetize a real human truth: people will play harder, watch longer, and give more when competition and generosity share a stage. But the format is locked at gala scale (venues, licenses, insurance, development staff), and every attempt to democratize it collides with a wall of law and a wall of ethics. The legal wall is the gambling triad: stake + contest + prize is wagering in most jurisdictions even when proceeds are charitable — and in the institution's own beachhead country, Cambodia, most gambling by citizens is criminal. The ethical wall belongs to this institution's own doctrine: money that moves *because someone won* is exchange contaminating gift, and a family gratitude economy that lets one household's treasury drain into another's on a claimed game outcome has built a laundering rail and called it fun.

This paper specifies the mechanism that takes the real thing — stakes, tension, a pot, a crowd — and removes the wager from under it, load-bearingly, at every layer. **B-Match℠** is a challenge grammar in which two players or teams agree to a physical contest, and: (1) **both stakes transfer unconditionally at acceptance** — the "match" is matching the *gift*, not risking it; the loser loses only the game; (2) stakes flow only to each player's own communal vessel (their family's shared pot, or the commons pool) — **stakes feed kitties; pots feed the commons**; (3) third parties add to a **patron pot** whose pledges are **charged when made** (commit-always) and whose only outcome-contingency is the **address** — which champion's *cause* receives it — never its existence; and (4) the winner's prize is the **broadcast dedication**: the victory speech, transmuted into naming where the gift goes and whom it honors. The design's legal core is stated as **the two-key separation**: the parties who pay (players) can win nothing; the parties whose money is address-contingent (patrons) have no agency in the outcome and receive nothing — no person anywhere holds both consideration and contingency, so no wager exists anywhere in the structure. The stake amount itself is set by a neutral intermediary as a ceremonial unit, so a challenge can never become a flex or a wealth-gate. Nine claims are enumerated and dedicated to the public domain; three predictions are pre-registered, including the design's own crowding-out wager, which the authors commit to publishing either way.

**Keywords:** charity tournament, gambling triad, wagering law, conditional pledge, walkathon grammar, charity streaming, sponsorship, gift economy, merit dedication, pattidāna, competitive altruism, defensive publication.

## 1 · The Problem: The Gala's Monopoly on Competitive Giving

The charity tournament works because it fuses three durable motives — play, status, and generosity — into one event where each amplifies the others. The golfer plays harder because a cause is watching; the donor gives more because a contest carries the ask; the crowd assembles for sport and leaves having funded a hospital wing. The format's effectiveness is not in question. Its *distribution* is: charity tournaments exist at gala scale and almost nowhere else. A family game night cannot hold one. Two friends at a park table cannot hold one. The reason is that the format's engine — money riding on a contest — is legally radioactive at every scale below the one that can afford lawyers.

The law's concern is the **gambling triad**: consideration (you paid to enter), chance or contest (an uncertain outcome), and prize (you may receive value if it breaks your way). Assemble all three in one person and you have a wager, and it does not stop being a wager because the proceeds are pious — charitable gaming is a *licensed exception* in most jurisdictions, not an exemption, and the licenses live at gala scale. The concern is not pedantry. In Cambodia — this institution's beachhead — the Law on the Suppression of Gambling (1996) outlaws unauthorized gambling, and the licensed casinos that the Law on the Management of Commercial Gambling (2020) regulates admit foreign nationals only; for Cambodian citizens, most gambling is prohibited outright. A "family-scale charity match" whose loser pays the winner's chosen recipient is, in the jurisdiction that matters most to this institution, a crime with extra steps.

The institution's own doctrine erects a second wall in the same place. This corpus's founding game is an anti-zero-sum children's game; its economic architecture routes all cross-family money through commons pass-through precisely so that no peer-to-peer transfer rail exists to be abused; and its design grammar holds a standing rule — **the play/currency wall** (coined in the companion sport paper) — that competition may generate story while only witnessed giving moves currency. A match in which the loser's account drains into the winner's family pot violates all three at once: it is zero-sum money between families inside an anti-zero-sum institution; it is a P2P transfer triggered by a claimed game outcome (a collusion and laundering rail wearing a game costume); and it is value moved by victory rather than by giving.

> **Current form.** The institution's design now also carries direct thank-tips between people and between families: a lateral gift layer beside the communal pool, in which each transfer is a giver's own act. The sentence above that says all cross-family money routes through commons pass-through is retained as written; the argument does not depend on it, because a loser-pays match would still be a cross-family transfer triggered by a claimed game outcome rather than by a giver's act, which no layer of the design permits.

So the design brief is exact: **keep everything that makes the charity tournament work — stakes, tension, a growing pot, a crowd, a winner's moment — while ensuring that no money anywhere in the structure is contingent, in its existence, on who wins.** What remains contestable, and how tension survives the surgery, is the substance of this paper.

## 2 · The Canonical Scene, and the Four-Line Grammar

**A park, two tables, a Saturday.** Two friends from different families agree to a best-of-three sey net match. The challenger picked the game; neither picked the amount — the app named a ceremonial stake, the same neutral unit it names for everyone. At the moment the challenge is accepted, both stakes move: each player's stake flows to that player's own family pot. The money is already gone, already given, already home; nothing that happens on the court can move it again. Word of the match travels; a dozen neighbors drift over; two local shops and several spectators pledge into the patron pot — each pledge charged on the spot, each pledgor choosing a champion's cause to back or "either way." The pot's total climbs on every phone like a tote board. The match is loud and close. When it ends, the winner does what winners of B-Matches do: takes the broadcast — the victory speech — and speaks the dedication: where the patron pot goes, and in whose honor. The crowd's phones carry the speech; the spectators thank and re-thank through the ordinary rails. The loser lost two games of sey. Every dollar present was a gift from the moment it appeared.

The mechanism's canonical compression is four lines, and the paper's architecture annotates them:

1. **Players match cause** — each names their vessel (own family pot or the commons pool); stakes transfer unconditionally at acceptance.
2. **Patrons pledge into pot** — committed when made; address-contested only.
3. **Winner broadcasts the dedication** — the victory speech names where the pot goes and whom it honors.
4. **Spectators thank** — rejoicing flows through the ordinary gratitude rails, never through match mechanics.

Readers of this corpus's tradition will recognize the sequence — give, back, dedicate, rejoice — as acts drawn from the classical bases of merit-making, performed in order (giving, dāna, twice; then dedication, pattidāna; then rejoicing, pattānumodanā); the observation is lineage only and carries no mechanism weight. Structurally, the match is a liturgy with a game inside it.

## 3 · The First Wall: Unconditional Matched Stakes

The foundational move is the redefinition of the stake. In a wager, matched stakes are *risked*: they sit in escrow, and the outcome decides who keeps them. In B-Match, matched stakes are **given**: at the moment of acceptance, both players' stakes transfer, irrevocably, to their designated vessels (§4). "Matching" the challenger means matching their *gift*, not their risk — the entry-fee grammar of every charity fun-run, applied bilaterally.

The single change collapses the whole hazard surface:

- **The gambling triad disassembles.** The players have paid consideration, but there is no prize: nothing returns to a player, and nothing moves on the outcome. Whatever else the event is, no player is wagering.
- **The laundering rail closes.** No sequence of real or claimed outcomes can move value from one player to another, because no outcome moves value at all. A "match" between colluders accomplishes nothing except two donations.
- **The loser's shame surface vanishes.** In loser-pays formats, defeat is compounded by visible financial loss — a humiliation mechanic that would be poison on a family surface and worse on a stream. Here the loser lost *only the game*; their stake went where they sent it before the first serve, to their own family's pot.
- **The stake becomes warm.** "My stake feeds my family, win or lose" is a materially different sentence from "I might lose my stake." The former is a reason to play; the latter is a reason to gamble.

**The stake amount is never the players' to set.** A challenger who names a stake is flexing; a required match that exceeds an opponent's means is a wealth-gate; and a stake ladder is a status economy. The neutral intermediary sets the stake as a **ceremonial unit** — small, standard, one-sentence-explainable, the same grammar this corpus already uses for gift-pledge sizing — so that the only thing a challenge can escalate is the game.

## 4 · Vessel Scoping: Stakes Feed Kitties, Pots Feed the Commons

Where may given money land? The design answers with one scoping rule per layer, and the rule is the second wall.

**Player stakes** flow to the player's **own communal vessel**: their family's shared pot, or the institution's commons pool, at the player's choice. Within one family (the game-night case) both players share a vessel, so both stakes land in the *same* pot — the family literally cannot lose its own match, and the competition is pure theater over an unconditional household gift. Across families, each stake is a self-to-own-vessel transfer: nothing crosses between households, which is what keeps the stake layer clean of the pass-through architecture's concerns — there is no cross-family flow to police because there is none at all.

**The patron pot** (§5) flows only to **commons-class causes** — never to any participant's own vessel. The asymmetry is deliberate and load-bearing, compressed in the design's slogan: *stakes feed kitties; pots feed the commons.* If the patron pot could land in the winner's family pot, the triad would silently reassemble — players pay (stakes), contest (the match), and the winner's *household* receives third-party money contingent on victory: a raffle with extra steps, and in the beachhead jurisdiction, worse. Scoped to the commons, the contested layer contains nothing any contestant can pocket, at any distance.

**Champion-a-cause.** For the pot's address contest to mean anything, the two sides must champion *different* causes. Eligible causes are non-self-adjacent commons vessels: the institution's pool; consecrated commons projects (in this corpus's product language, an orphaned shrine's restoration); communal capacity funds. Two classes are excluded by name: any vessel adjacent to a participant's own household (the triad, again), and **unconditional-support classes** — in this institution's architecture, support flowing to monastics and their communities is firewalled as unconditional by constitutional rule, and a cause whose funding rides on a game outcome is by that fact disqualified from being contested. Nothing sacred may be made to win a rally for its lunch.

## 5 · The Patron Pot: Commit-Always, Address-Contested

The stake layer, made unconditional, deliberately drains the outcome of financial meaning — and with it, some tension. The design restores tension at the only layer where it is safe: third-party money, governed by two rules.

**Commit-always.** A patron's pledge is **charged when made**. It is a completed gift from the moment of pledging; no outcome can create it, cancel it, or return it. What the patron chooses at pledge time is a *routing preference*: "my addition rides with this champion's cause" — meaning *if that champion wins, my pledge lands at their cause; if they lose, it follows my declared fallback* (the winner's cause, or the commons pool either way). The refundable form — "charge me only if my champion wins" — is barred by name: a pledge that returns on a loss is a stake the patron can lose, which resurrects betting psychology (backing favorites, hedging, odds-thinking) inside a donation interface. Under commit-always, there is nothing to hedge: the pot is already given; the game decides only where it goes.

**Address-contested only.** The consequence, stated as the design's deepest structural fact: **once any money enters B-Match — stake or pledge — its existence is unconditional; outcomes contest only addresses.** The match is a contest over the *routing* of gifts that have already been given, never over whether giving occurs. This is the play/currency wall's precise application: the game generates story and steers addresses; the giving all happened at commitment time, witnessed, through the ordinary rails.

**The two-key separation.** The legal analysis compresses to one sentence, offered as this paper's most portable contribution: **no person in the structure holds both keys — the parties who paid (players) can receive nothing and their money cannot move on the outcome; the parties whose money is outcome-routed (patrons) paid nothing *for a chance* (their gift is complete regardless), control nothing, and receive nothing.** Consideration and contingency never meet in the same person; a wager requires them to meet; therefore no wager exists anywhere in the structure — not for players, not for patrons, not for the house (there is no house: the intermediary takes nothing, holds no float, and profits from no outcome). What remains is the legal genus of the **performance-contingent charitable pledge** — the walkathon's dollars-per-mile, the pledge-per-point drive, the bid war of charity streaming — a genre so ordinary that school children administer it. Counsel must still review sweepstakes, promotion, and money-transmission surfaces before launch (§10); but the structure's center of gravity has been moved, deliberately, from the most regulated category of money-in-games to one of the least regulated.

**Sponsor discipline.** Brand and business pledges are welcome and governed by two bars: **pot-only, never placement** — a brand's pledge is a gift that enlarges the pot and buys nothing: no placement, surfacing, targeting, or attention (the institution's standing anti-ad-spend rule); and **no brand money on matches involving minors** — a family game night's pot accepts individual patrons only, because a brand raising the stakes on a children's stream is child-directed advertising with extra steps. The intermediary also caps patron pots so that a pot never dwarfs the combined stakes: the unconditional stakes are the mechanism, the pot is the theater (P-M2, §8; §10 item 4). The intermediary never asks anyone to pledge, or to give to the commons pool; the pot's running total is shown as a fact, with no goal, prompt or suggested amount, and the commons pool is never a pre-selected address. Large pots trigger proportional outcome-integrity requirements (witnessed or streamed attestation at intermediary-set thresholds): where third-party money rides on addresses, match-fixing becomes worth something, and integrity must scale with the pot.

**The vocabulary wall.** The surface speaks the language of pledging, never of betting: *patron pot, pledge, back, dedicate* — never *bet, wager, odds, payout, purse, jackpot*, and not "prize money." Regulators, journalists, and users all read the words before the mechanism; a clean structure described in casino vocabulary will be treated as a casino, and deserves to be.

## 6 · The Prize: The Broadcast Dedication

What, then, does the winner win? The design's answer completes the transmutation: the winner takes the oldest object in sports culture — the microphone — and the victory speech becomes the **dedication**. The winner names, before the assembled circle and any watching stream: where the patron pot goes (their championed cause, by the contest's result) and *in whose honor* — a person, a memory, an occasion. In the corpus's inherited vocabulary this is pattidāna, the dedication of merit; in purely secular terms it is the one prize that is simultaneously real (the pot's address was genuinely contested and genuinely won), weightless (nothing pocketable exists), and generative (the speech is itself a gift — the honor it names lands on someone). *What you win is the right to give the gift a name.*

Spectator response — the rejoicing, the thanks, the small gifts a moved crowd sends — flows exclusively through the institution's ordinary gratitude rails, exactly as any witnessed kindness would. The match apparatus mints nothing, awards nothing, and maintains no reputation: no aura, points, rankings, ladders, or head-to-head records attach to winning, ever. (The sport's own witness architecture, including its refusal of player statistics, is the companion paper's subject; the challenge layer inherits it whole.)

**A dual-currency event without conversion.** One integration deserves its own paragraph. In the institution's time-currency (the Chronicle), a pledged hour may be *redeemed as* a B-Match — the match appears on the curated togetherness menu, and two people spend a promised hour across a net. In that event, three currencies of meaning move in one afternoon with zero conversion among them: the **hour** is spent as co-presence (the time-currency's only redemption form, per the corpus's co-presence publication); the **stakes** flow as unconditional money-gifts to the players' vessels; and the **victory** moves neither — it moves the pot's address and the dedication's honor. The corpus's coupler doctrine (money and time as non-convertible currencies joined only by a neutral intermediary) here acquires its first *event-shaped* instance: a single game in which both currencies are present, both flow, and no conversion event exists even in principle. The wall between the currencies is not merely maintained during the match; the match is a demonstration that the wall can host a party.

## 7 · What the Design Refuses

For the record, as prior art in refusal form — an implementation of "charity challenge" mechanics that includes any of the following is *not* this mechanism:

1. **Loser-pays in any form** — any transfer whose direction or occurrence depends on the outcome.
2. **Escrowed or refundable stakes** — stakes held pending outcome, or pledges returned on a loss.
3. **Winner-adjacent pot routing** — any contested money landing in any vessel a contestant or their household draws from.
4. **Player-set or open-ended stake amounts** — stake ladders, side bets, or challenger-named sums.
5. **Odds, prices, or markets** — any surface expressing outcome probability as a number attached to money.
6. **Victory-linked value** — aura, reputation, rankings, or economic capacity minted or moved by winning.
7. **Contestable sacred support** — unconditional-support classes (monastic support and equivalents) offered as championable causes.
8. **Brand pledges on minors' matches**, and any sponsorship purchasing placement or targeting rather than pot.

## 8 · Pre-Registered Predictions

All at n = 0; instruments await pilots.

- **P-M1 (honor is enough).** The design's central psychological wager: dedication-prize matches sustain repeat engagement — a majority of family and friend groups that hold one staked B-Match hold another within 60 days. If honor-contingency proves insufficient to carry the format without pocketable prizes, the format fails and the authors will say so.
- **P-M2 (the pot behaves).** Under intermediary capping, patron pots remain theater rather than gravity: the median pot stays within an order of magnitude of the median combined stakes, and no drift toward pot-maximization dynamics (solicitation pressure, champion-shopping) appears. Publish-either-way; a misbehaving pot triggers the cap's tightening and a design revision.
- **P-M3 (the crowding wager — publish either way).** Staked matches do not crowd out unstaked play: families that adopt B-Match maintain or increase their rate of ordinary, unstaked games and ordinary, unprompted giving. The overjustification literature (§9) predicts risk here; the design bets that *unconditional* stakes — which never pay the player — sit outside the reward-contingency that drives crowding-out. This is the design's honest exposure to its own tradition's hardest finding, and the result will be published whichever way it falls.

## 9 · Prior Art, Engaged Generously

**The pledge-per-performance genre.** Walkathons, readathons, jump-rope-for-heart, pledge-per-mile and pledge-per-point drives: charitable giving conditioned on performance quantities, administered for decades by schools and churches without gambling taint — the legal genus this design claims for outcome-routed pledges. Charity streaming (notably Games Done Quick and the Twitch charity ecosystem) advanced the genre to real-time: donations steer in-game choices, bid wars contest options, totals climb on screen, and — decisively for this design — **all money is kept regardless of which option wins; the contest is over the choice, not the gift.** Commit-always generalizes that insight from menu choices to match outcomes.

**The charity match.** Celebrity charity matches and "loser donates to winner's charity" bets are the near-ancestor and the instructive anti-example: when the *loser's* payment is outcome-triggered, the structure is a wager in form, whatever the legal treatment of any particular celebrity example. The professional form — tournament purses paid to the winner's designated charity — is the two-key separation avant la lettre at gala scale; this design's contribution is the full grammar that survives at family scale, where no tournament license, insurer, or counsel attends.

**Gaming law.** The gambling triad (consideration/chance/prize) as organizing doctrine; skill-versus-chance jurisprudence; charitable-gaming licensing regimes; sweepstakes law's no-consideration branch; and Cambodia's Law on the Suppression of Gambling (1996) with the Law on the Management of Commercial Gambling (2020), under which Cambodian citizens are barred from most gambling — the constraint that binds this design. The design is architected so that the analysis never reaches the skill-chance question: with no prize to any payer, the triad fails at its prize element.

**The institution's own corpus.** The incommensurability-preserving coupler (non-convertible dual currencies; the Terra collapse as the coupled-with-convertibility anti-example); co-presence-gated redemption (the hour spendable only as shared presence — the menu on which this format appears); *The Quest That Mints Nothing* (need-compiled questlines; the mints-nothing boundary this format inherits); the four-channel disbursement grammar (all pool flows human-initiated or communal-vessel-bound — the patron pot's commons scoping composes with it). The division of claims is stated in the Prior-Art and Non-Assertion Statement.

**Competitive altruism.** The behavioral literature on generosity under observation and status competition (competitive philanthropy, donor walls, potlatch scholarship) documents both the engine this design harnesses — giving intensifies in contest — and the pathology it guards against (giving as dominance display). The ceremonial stake unit and the pot cap are the guards: the only escalation channel open to status is the quality of play and the grace of the dedication.

**Crowding-out.** Titmuss on blood; Lepper–Greene–Nisbett on children's intrinsic motivation; Gneezy–Rustichini's day-care fine (a price attached to a moral act converts it to a commodity); Frey on motivation crowding. This literature is the reason for P-M3's publish-either-way commitment and for the design's care that no player is ever *paid*: the crowding mechanism runs through reward contingency, which the unconditional stake severs by construction.

## 10 · Honest Limits

*This section is deliberately free of lineage, resonance, and metaphor.*

1. **The legal surface is unverified.** No counsel has reviewed this design. The two-key separation is an architectural argument, not an opinion of counsel; sweepstakes and promotions law, money-transmission licensing, charitable-solicitation registration, and the Cambodian statute's application to *any* game-adjacent money flow all require jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction review before launch. The authors assert that the structure is built to survive that review, not that it has.
2. **n = 0.** No B-Match has been played. All predictions are pre-registered hopes with instruments attached.
3. **Honor-contingency may be insufficient.** The dedication may not carry enough stakes-tension to sustain the format (P-M1's subject). The patron pot partially compensates at the third-party layer; whether the combination suffices is empirical.
4. **The pot cap is a judgment call.** "Theater, not gravity" is a design intent without a derived threshold; the intermediary's cap-setting rubric will be tuned on pilot data and is a plausible failure point in both directions (too low starves the tension; too high invites the pot-maximization dynamics P-M2 watches for).
5. **Outcome-integrity is deterrence, not prevention.** Attestation thresholds raise the cost of fixing a match to steer a pot; they do not make it impossible. The design's consolation is that the theft's proceeds can only reach a commons cause — a strange crime — but strange crimes occur.
6. **The crowding wager is a wager.** P-M3 may fall against the design. The commitment is to publish and revise, not to be right.
7. **Cultural fit is assumed, not shown.** The dedication rite reads naturally in the institution's founding culture; whether a victory speech that gives everything away carries the same charge elsewhere is unknown.

## 11 · Claims

The following mechanisms and architectures, individually and in combination, are published as prior art and dedicated to the public domain:

1. **Unconditional matched stakes.** A challenge structure in which all participant stakes transfer irrevocably to designated beneficiary vessels at challenge acceptance, prior to play, such that no participant payment's existence, amount, or direction depends on outcome.
2. **Vessel scoping.** The two-layer routing rule: participant stakes flow only to the participant's own communal vessel (shared household pot or commons pool); all outcome-routed third-party money flows only to non-participant-adjacent commons causes — *stakes feed kitties; pots feed the commons.*
3. **The two-key separation.** The structural guarantee that no party holds both consideration and outcome-contingency: paying parties can receive nothing and their payments cannot move on outcome; parties whose money is outcome-routed have completed their gift unconditionally, hold no agency in the outcome, and receive nothing.
4. **Commit-always pledging with address-contest.** A third-party pledge instrument charged irrevocably at pledge time, carrying a routing designation (a championed cause) and a declared fallback, such that contest outcomes select among pre-committed charitable addresses and can never create, cancel, or return a pledge; expressly excluding refundable or outcome-charged forms.
5. **Champion-a-cause eligibility.** The cause-eligibility rule set: contested addresses must be non-self-adjacent commons vessels; unconditional-support classes (constitutionally firewalled beneficiaries, exemplarily monastic support) are structurally excluded from contest.
6. **The intermediary-set ceremonial stake.** Stake amounts set solely by a neutral intermediary as small standardized units on a coarse public rubric, with participants unable to set, raise, or ladder stakes — foreclosing flex, wealth-gating, and stake escalation as social mechanics.
7. **The dedication-right prize.** A prize consisting solely of the broadcast dedication: the winner's right to announce the contested pot's routing and to name the honoree — with all spectator response flowing through ordinary gratitude rails external to the match apparatus, and no reputation, ranking, or value minted by victory.
8. **Placement-blind sponsorship with a minors bar.** Sponsor participation restricted to pot contributions under commit-always terms, purchasing no placement, surfacing, or targeting; with brand pledges excluded from any match involving minors; and outcome-integrity attestation scaling with pot size.
9. **The dual-currency non-conversion event.** The composition of a time-currency co-presence redemption with a money-currency challenge in a single event such that the hour is spent only as shared presence, money moves only as unconditional gifts, no conversion between currencies occurs or is expressible, and outcome affects only address and honor.

## 12 · Lineage, and the Close

The tradition this institution draws from remembers generosity as, on occasion, a contest. The Vinaya tells how Anāthapiṇḍika covered Prince Jeta's park with coins to buy it for the community; the Dhammapada commentary tells of the *asadisadāna*, the incomparable offering, in which King Pasenadi of Kosala and the citizens of Sāvatthī outgave one another in successive alms-givings to the Buddha and the monks until the queen, Mallikā, devised an offering none could match: a race in which every lap is a donation. What that lineage understood, and what the gala-scale charity tournament rediscovered by accident, is that rivalry is not the opposite of generosity but one of its great amplifiers — *if* the structure guarantees that what the rivals are racing to do is give. The mechanism above is that guarantee, built for a card table, a driveway, and a net strung between two chairs. The match ends; the winner takes the microphone; and the speech that follows is the only trophy in the building: the name of a cause, the name of an honoree, and a pot that was everyone's gift before the first point was played.
