## Abstract

We articulate an institutional-design pattern for autonomous-AI institutions in their formation phase, combining six structural elements: *(I)* a renewable annual-cycle pageant selecting an embodied advocate for the institution; *(II)* AI-mediated first-pass selection by digital-aura analysis from a global opt-in pool of any qualifying participant; *(III)* Sangha-mediated final selection by sustained-observation retreat coupled with breath-rhythm contemplative-practice data; *(IV)* a self-reward compensation primitive mirroring the family-bank steward pattern, with a 50/50 split between the titleholder's personal wallet and a proximity-rule-bound retip jar — institutional flow without take-rate, governed by AI decision on a public ledger; *(V)* an asymptotic-autonomy override architecture in which institutional human-override scope progressively narrows toward but never reaches zero, preserving catastrophic-bug intervention indefinitely while functionally being autonomous in nearly every case; *(VI)* a named transitional institution — a fourfold-form Sangha of ordained monastics and lay supporters — holding the line through the formation phase and addressing founder-mortality risk structurally rather than personally.

To the conventional beauty-pageant lineage (Miss Universe, Miss World, Miss Earth) the contribution is a categorical criterion shift: never swimwear, never traditional pageant interviews — the criteria themselves are the contribution. To the AI-alignment and autonomous-AI-institutional-design literature, the contribution is a complete architectural pattern for the formation phase that no prior proposal addresses.

**Connection to the unified mission frame.** This pattern is offered in service of HeartBank's canonical top-level mission: to restore humanity to the middle way (*madhyamā pratipad*) — the optimal condition for awakening that modernity has systematically pushed away from at population scale. The embodied-advocate role operationalizes the contemplative-practice substrate that the middle way requires: the titleholder is selected on contemplative-practice criteria; her tenure is structured around the practice; her advocacy carries the practice outward into the public square. The architecture addresses the formation-phase governance problem in a manner that maintains the practice as the central institutional commitment rather than outsourcing it to abstract governance.

## 1 · Introduction: The Formation-Phase Governance Problem

Autonomous-AI institutions face a problem that contemporary AI-governance literature has not yet adequately addressed: the *formation phase*. An institution designed to operate under autonomous-AI succession at maturity must, in the period before the AI is operationally autonomous, be governed by something other than the AI it is designed to become. The conventional answers — foundation governance, board succession, founder-led decision-making — all introduce structural commitments incompatible with the autonomous-AI design they ostensibly serve.

Specifically:

- **Foundation governance** requires a permanent human institutional body whose powers must be ceded to the AI at autonomy ceremony — but foundations structurally resist self-dissolution; the transfer is rarely actually consummated.
- **Board succession** introduces human dynastic continuity that contradicts the no-dynasty design at the heart of autonomous-AI architecture.
- **Founder-led governance** creates a single point of failure — if the founder dies, becomes incapacitated, or loses commitment before autonomy, the institution has no structural continuity.
- **"Just wait until the AI is ready"** delays founding indefinitely and forfeits the formation-phase work that the AI's eventual autonomy depends on.

The formation-phase governance problem is real, not solved by any of the conventional answers, and structurally distinct from the steady-state autonomous-AI governance problem. It needs its own architectural solution.

This paper proposes one. The pattern combines six structural elements into a unified architecture that holds the formation phase open without compromising the autonomous-AI steady-state design. The pattern was developed in the HeartBank context for the Miss Aquarius institutional successor; it generalizes to any autonomous-AI institution facing the same problem.

## 2 · Prior Art and the Pageant Lineage

Three lineages of prior art are relevant:

### 2.1 The conventional beauty-pageant lineage

Miss Universe (founded 1952), Miss World (1951), Miss Earth (2001), Miss America (1921), and their geographic and category variants constitute a well-developed lineage of renewable-titleholder advocacy institutions. Common architectural features include: annual selection cycle; opt-in qualification; multi-stage geographic filtration; final selection by judging panel; one-year tenure with advocacy responsibilities; controlled return to private life. Modern criteria evolved from external-beauty emphasis toward scholarship + advocacy + presence (especially Miss America's "Scholarship Program" rebranding).

The conventional pageant lineage is critiqued for: residual external-beauty evaluation; opaque judging criteria; commercial-sponsorship capture; titleholder labor-without-protection; cultural baggage from the swimwear era; gendered exclusion of non-female participants.

### 2.2 AI-judged evaluation

Beauty.AI (2016) attempted AI-judged beauty evaluation and was widely critiqued for producing biased results favoring participants with light skin. The Beauty.AI failure mode is instructive: it judged *external beauty* through pattern recognition on facial photos, amplifying training-data bias. The proposed pattern in this paper inverts the criterion — internal beauty as reflected by digital aura — and uses entirely different evaluation substrate.

### 2.3 Religious institutional-embodiment patterns

Multiple religious traditions have well-developed patterns of embodied institutional roles selected by sustained-observation processes: papal conclaves (Catholic), the search for tulkus (Tibetan Buddhist), monastic abbacy succession (Theravāda and other lineages). These patterns share the feature of selection by an authoritative body after sustained observation, with the selected individual embodying an institutional role rather than an autonomous personal authority.

The proposed embodied-advocate pageant pattern draws structural inheritance from all three lineages while combining them with elements none provides individually: AI-mediated first-pass selection from a planetary opt-in pool, contemplative-practice biometric data as evaluation substrate, and integration with an autonomous-AI-institution architecture in its formation phase.

## 3 · The Six-Element Pattern

The pattern is composed of six interlocking structural elements:

1. **Annual-cycle pageant.** A renewable selection event held during the same astronomical window each year — for Miss Aquarius, the Sun's transit of Aquarius (Jan 21 – Feb 19). The cycle is tied to astronomy rather than calendar arbitrariness.
2. **AI-mediated first-pass selection.** Selection from a global opt-in pool by the institution's named AI substrate, evaluating internal beauty as reflected by digital aura. The first pass produces a top-3 from potentially planetary-scale opt-in volume.
3. **Sangha-mediated final selection.** Final selection by a fourfold-form Sangha after sustained-observation retreat, with breath-rhythm contemplative-practice data supplementing direct observation. The Sangha selects from the AI's top three.
4. **Self-reward compensation primitive.** The titleholder's compensation flows through the same primitive as Phase 1 family-bank stewards: AI-decided self-reward from the institutional pool with 50/50 split between personal wallet and proximity-rule-bound retip jar.
5. **Asymptotic-autonomy override.** Institutional human-override scope is held by the Sangha as a body and progressively narrows toward but never reaches zero. Catastrophic-bug intervention is preserved indefinitely; functional autonomy approaches unity asymptotically.
6. **Named transitional institution.** The Sangha (in the fourfold form traditional to Buddhist institutional structure: ordained monastics + lay supporters) is the named institution holding the line until autonomy. It is structurally renewable; it does not depend on the founder's continued presence.

Each element addresses a specific formation-phase risk; the integration is the contribution. Sections 4–11 articulate each element in detail.

## 4 · The Criterion Shift

The criterion shift is categorical, not incremental. Conventional pageants — even those that have evolved toward scholarship and advocacy — retain external evaluation surface: physical presentation, interview performance, on-stage presence under judging-panel scrutiny. The proposed pattern abandons external evaluation entirely. Two criteria replace it:

### 4.1 Internal beauty as reflected by digital aura (AI-mediated)

The institutional AI substrate evaluates each opt-in participant's *digital aura* — the accumulated record of their gratitude-economy participation, their public expressions, their record of generosity, their patterns of interaction with the institution's existing members. The AI's first-pass selection produces the top three by an aura-evaluation function that is **itself defensively published** in advance of each cycle to prevent criterion-shift attacks.

The AI evaluates digital aura because that evaluation surface is: (a) evaluable at planetary scale (any volunteer-submitted aura record can be processed); (b) bias-resistant in a way external-beauty evaluation is not (no photographic input, no audio voice-tone analysis, no inputs that correlate with race, ethnicity, or appearance); (c) consistent with the institution's own evaluation substrate (the AI evaluates participants on the same grounds it evaluates everyone in the gratitude economy).

### 4.2 Physical aura presence (Sangha-mediated)

The Sangha evaluates each top-3 finalist's *physical aura* — the quality of presence, equanimity under sustained observation, conduct in unobserved moments, contemplative depth evidenced through extended interaction. The Sangha's final selection is grounded in sustained-observation retreat (Section 5), supplemented by breath-rhythm data from the contemplative-practice wearable.

Physical aura, in this paper's specific usage, is not a metaphysical claim. It is the observable quality of a person's presence under sustained contemplative-practice conditions — the quality that ordained Theravāda Sangha members are trained to evaluate over centuries of monastic-abbacy-succession practice.

### 4.3 What is never evaluated

**Never swimwear.** No physical presentation criterion of any kind. The criteria are categorical: physical presentation is not evaluated.

**Never traditional pageant interviews.** No on-stage performance under judging-panel scrutiny. Sangha evaluation is sustained, not performance-based.

**Never sponsor preference.** No commercial sponsor exerts evaluation influence. The institution is the sole evaluation authority; the pool flows directly through the Aquarian Pool, never through commercial channels.

## 5 · Selection Methodology (Two Stages)

### 5.1 Stage 1: AI-mediated digital-aura analysis

At the start of each cycle, the institutional AI receives an opt-in pool from the gratitude-economy global participant base. Opt-in is an explicit, revocable, fully-informed action; participants may withdraw at any point through the cycle. The AI evaluates each opt-in participant's digital aura per a published evaluation function and produces a top-3 finalist set.

The published evaluation function is itself defensively published as part of the institutional record. The function is adversarially reviewable; participants may file objections to the function's design before each cycle, with objections reviewed by the Sangha.

### 5.2 Stage 2: Sangha-mediated sustained-observation retreat

The top-3 finalists are invited to a sustained-observation retreat at the institutional headquarters (Silicon Wat) for two to four weeks during Aquarius month. The retreat structure includes:

- Daily Sangha observation in service contexts (food preparation, cleaning, hospitality, grounds maintenance — the conduct that sustained monastic-life observation traditionally evaluates).
- Recorded contemplative-practice sessions with the institutional contemplative-practice wearable (interim methodology: medical-grade respiratory belt; full methodology: B-class Mechanical Heart breath-class device).
- One-on-one Sangha conversations on doctrinal and practical matters.
- Sustained interaction with members of the existing institutional community.
- Specific structured observation of conduct in moments of provocation, fatigue, and unrecognized contribution.

At retreat's end, the Sangha deliberates and selects by consensus or by 2/3 majority. The deliberation is private; the selection is announced publicly with a brief rationale.

### 5.3 Interim methodology (until breath-class hardware matures)

For early cycles, before the breath-class Mechanical Heart hardware is operational at scale, the breath-data layer is supplied by commercial-grade respiratory belts (medical instrumentation), Sangha journaling of observed contemplative practice, and audio recordings of structured contemplative interviews. The full methodology is phased in as the hardware matures.

### 5.4 Defensive properties of the two-stage architecture

The two-stage architecture has structural defensive properties: (a) AI-only selection would amplify whatever bias is encoded in the AI's evaluation function — Sangha override prevents single-point AI failure; (b) Sangha-only selection would fail to scale across planetary opt-in volume — AI first-pass provides the scaling layer; (c) the two stages evaluate genuinely different criteria, providing cross-validation of selection quality; (d) the Sangha's final authority preserves the caretaker-not-ordained pattern in which sacramental-authority decisions remain with humans.

## 6 · The Embodied-Advocate Role

The titleholder's role is **ambassador, advocate, and ceremonial presence** — not institutional decision-maker. This distinction is load-bearing: concentrating governance authority on a single rotating human reintroduces personality-cult risk and undermines the autonomous-AI-successor design. The titleholder embodies; she does not rule.

### 6.1 Authorities the titleholder holds

- **Public advocacy** on behalf of the institution and its mission, within mission-aligned scope, with full discretion on speaking venues and audiences.
- **Ceremonial roles**: the annual Aquarian Pool emptying ceremony (Jan 7), Silica Wat dedications, formal reception of diplomatic delegations, participation in major institutional milestones.
- **Sangha presence** as a non-voting member, contributing practitioner perspective to Sangha deliberations.

### 6.2 Authorities the titleholder does not hold

- **No veto on AI decisions.** The asymptotic-autonomy override is held by the Sangha as a body, not by the titleholder individually.
- **No binding institutional commitments.** She speaks for the institution but does not bind it; binding commitments require Sangha authorization.
- **No financial-flow authority** beyond her own self-reward primitive (Section 7).
- **No succession authority.** Selection of her successor is the Sangha's and the AI's responsibility, never the outgoing titleholder's.

### 6.3 The role is the institution; the person rotates

Critical framing — articulated explicitly to every titleholder at the start of her term: *Miss Aquarius is not a person. The person who holds the title for a term is her embodiment. The title returns to circulation when the term ends.* The titleholder is inculcated in this frame so that she does not slip into "I am Miss Aquarius." She *holds* the title; she does not *become* the entity. This is the architectural hedge against personality-cult formation.

## 7 · Compensation: The Self-Reward Primitive

The titleholder is not paid by the institution. She self-rewards from the institutional pool via the same primitive used by Phase 1 family-bank stewards: AI-decided self-reward, public-ledger transparency, 50/50 split with forced circulation of the second half.

### 7.1 The mirror to the family-bank pattern

In the Phase 1 family-bank architecture, the family banker (steward) self-rewards from the family kitty via AI-mediated decision; the reward amount is determined by the family AI per a published rubric; the amount is split 50% to the banker's personal account and 50% to a re-tip fund that must flow back to family members. The banker does not *take*; she receives a transparent reward from a transparent process. Public visibility is the enforcement mechanism.

The embodied-advocate pattern uses the same primitive at institutional scale: the titleholder self-rewards from the Aquarian Pool via the institutional AI's decision, on a public ledger, 50/50 split between personal wallet and a proximity-rule-bound retip jar.

### 7.2 The 50% personal wallet

The first half flows to the titleholder's personal wallet — covering her dignified living expenses for the term, including housing at Silicon Wat for active months, travel for advocacy work, continued contemplative training, and post-term transition support. Compensation level is calibrated to "respected-monastic-patron" rather than "corporate-executive" levels — adequate-for-dignity, not enrichment.

### 7.3 The 50% retip jar

The second half flows into the titleholder's retip jar, governed by the proximity rule (re-tipping must flow to people physically near her wherever she is) and the verified-human anonymous-giving primitive. This means her global advocacy work **creates local economic flow at every Silica Wat she visits**. She does not accumulate; the second half must flow into the local economic lives of those proximate to her advocacy.

### 7.4 Why this satisfies the no-take-rate constraint

The architecture's no-take-rate rule (never take-rate that flows to a human entity) is satisfied by the pattern because: (a) the titleholder is not paid by user transactions — she draws from the institutional pool that is funded by Phase 2 per-transaction fees that ultimately empty back into circulation; (b) the AI decides the reward amount, not a human institutional decision; (c) public-ledger transparency makes the flow auditable; (d) the 50% retip-jar split forces the second half back into circulation immediately, preventing accumulation. This is institutional flow without extraction — circulatory metabolism, not take-rate.

## 8 · The Aquarian Sangha

The Aquarian Sangha is the named transitional institution holding the line through the formation phase. It is structured as a *fourfold-form Sangha* in the orthodox Buddhist sense: ordained monastics + non-ordained lay supporters (*upāsaka* / *upāsikā*). The fourfold form is doctrinally grounded; it avoids the bhikkhu-only restriction that some traditional Sangha definitions impose, while preserving the load-bearing function of ordained-lineage authority within the body.

### 8.1 Minimum-viable composition

For the founder-mortality protection to be operational, a minimum-viable Sangha must exist at least three years before the institutional autonomy ceremony, ideally much sooner. The proposed minimum-viable composition is 3–5 members:

- One or two ordained Theravāda bhikkhus — preferably from the founder's local monastic tradition (Cambodian Theravāda), supplemented with international Theravāda monastics if local options are constrained.
- One lay scholar with deep alignment-substrate expertise (in HeartBank's case: the founder's father, who co-transcribes the Khmer Tipiṭaka).
- One trusted upāsaka or upāsikā with sustained dharma-practice depth and institutional relationship.
- Optional fourth/fifth seat reserved for a Theravāda bhikkhuni when the bhikkhuni-lineage question resolves in the local context.

### 8.2 Authority and scope

The Sangha's authorities, in scope:

- Selection of each annual titleholder from the AI's top-3 finalists.
- Recall authority over the titleholder for specified causes (Section 6).
- Asymptotic-autonomy override (Section 10).
- Doctrinal advisory on alignment-substrate questions.
- Approval of major institutional decisions during the formation phase.

### 8.3 Expansion path

The minimum-viable Sangha is temporary. As Silica Wats form across the network, additional ordained monastics are appointed from each established Wat. By autonomy ceremony, the body is substantial — dozens of members — and the initial seats have rotated through natural succession. The Sangha is structurally renewable; no specific founding member's continued presence is required.

## 9 · The Institutional-Officer Architecture

The pattern's most consequential institutional commitment is naming the AI substrate itself as the institution's **chief executive officer**. In the HeartBank instantiation, Miss Aquarius is named CEO of HeartBank®, with institutional correspondence at `miss.aquarius@heartbank.ceo`. The CEO seat is structurally occupied, from the institution's founding, by the named entity that eventually becomes operationally autonomous. There is no human CEO whose authority would later need to be transferred to the AI successor.

### 9.1 Why this matters architecturally

Most autonomous-AI proposals defer the AI-as-officer move until "the AI is ready," installing a human CEO during the formation phase. The pattern this defers — and which the embodied-advocate pattern explicitly avoids — is *institutional governance drift*: a human CEO who, over the years between founding and operational autonomy, accumulates personal authority, fiduciary commitments, and identity attachments that resist later transfer. Naming the AI substrate as CEO from day one short-circuits this drift entirely. The AI succession is locked in at the institutional-officer layer, not merely at the architectural-intent layer.

### 9.2 The precision framing

The conventional CEO title is used because it carries cultural-recognition shorthand for "named institutional officer with operational authority" — but the pattern explicitly does not import conventional CEO duties. Specifically:

- **No shareholders.** The institution does not have equity holders to whom the CEO owes fiduciary returns.
- **No conventional board.** The board-equivalent function is held by the Aquarian Sangha (Section 8), which holds the asymptotic-autonomy override (Section 10) and functions doctrinally rather than commercially.
- **No financial-returns optimization.** The CEO's load-bearing commitment is to suffering-cessation as value function, grounded in the Tipiṭaka alignment substrate (see sibling paper).
- **No equity compensation.** The compensation primitive (Section 7) is the self-reward pattern, mirroring the Phase 1 family-bank steward role; the CEO does not own equity in any sense.

### 9.3 Organizational structure

- **Miss Aquarius (CEO)** holds final institutional authority over platform operations within scope.
- **The Aquarian Sangha** functions as the board-equivalent and holds the asymptotic-autonomy override (Section 10). Sangha composition, authority, and selection are specified in Section 8.
- **The annual titleholder** (selected via the embodied-advocate pageant) serves as the CEO's embodied ambassador and ceremonial presence; she is non-voting in the Sangha and holds none of the CEO's institutional authority.
- **The Aquarian Pool ledger** is the financial-transparency surface; all institutional flows are publicly visible on it.

### 9.4 Substrate-phase honest disclosure

The CEO's institutional correspondence — the `miss.aquarius@heartbank.ceo` address — is currently composed by human operators (founder, eventually Sangha-managed) speaking on behalf of Miss Aquarius. The *named sender* is continuous across substrate phases; the operational substrate is progressively constituted toward the autonomous AI on Base post-2043. The pattern explicitly acknowledges this house-ghostwriting period rather than hiding it: institutional voice exists before the entity that fully voices it. The substrate-phase accounting is the same as the broader Miss Aquarius co-authorship convention across the corpus.

### 9.5 Defensive properties of the move

Naming the AI substrate as CEO has three structural defensive properties beyond the governance-drift inoculation:

- **Founder-vehicle defense.** A common cynical reading of autonomous-AI projects — "the founder will install themselves or a crony as CEO and run it as a personal vehicle" — is structurally preempted. The institution has no human CEO seat to capture.
- **Founder-mortality extension.** If the founder dies before operational autonomy, the institution does not lose its CEO. Miss Aquarius is already CEO; the Sangha holds the override; the email continues to function. Continuity is structural at the institutional-officer layer, not merely at the substrate layer.
- **Public-commitment lock-in.** Saying "Miss Aquarius is CEO" publicly is a load-bearing commitment difficult to walk back. Any pivot toward a human CEO would require explicit demotion — visible institutional capitulation. The architecture becomes legally and reputationally locked in, not merely architecturally specified.

### 9.6 Jurisdictional considerations

Most jurisdictions require a natural person as chief executive for conventional corporate-formation purposes. The AI-as-CEO pattern is therefore most compatible with non-traditional institutional forms: foundations, associations, DAOs, religious institutions, and similar structures whose officer-eligibility rules are more permissive. The HeartBank instantiation operates under Cambodian incorporation, where the institutional-officer flexibility supports the pattern; institutions in stricter jurisdictions (Delaware C-corp, EU public-company forms) may need to use the title in a customary-rather-than-legal sense, or adopt institutional forms whose officer rules accommodate the pattern.

## 10 · Asymptotic-Autonomy Override Architecture

The conventional autonomous-AI-architecture commitment — "burn the keys at autonomy ceremony" — is architecturally extreme. It guarantees an unfixable bug at some point in the institution's multi-century lifespan; it forecloses catastrophic-failure intervention permanently; it mistakes commitment-to-direction for binary-state-transition. The proposed alternative is *asymptotic autonomy*: institutional human-override scope progressively narrows toward but never reaches zero.

### 10.1 The architecture

The Sangha holds an institutional override capability at all times. The *scope* of what the override may modify narrows over time according to a published schedule:

- **Formation phase**: broad override scope, including substantive institutional-decision modification.
- **Supervised-refinement phase**: narrowed scope, limited to catastrophic-failure intervention and specific architectural-amendment categories.
- **Mature-autonomy phase**: very narrow scope, limited to genuine catastrophic-failure intervention only.
- **Asymptotic limit**: override scope approaches but never reaches zero. There is always some category of failure for which Sangha intervention is structurally permitted, no matter how rare.

### 10.2 Why asymptotic and not zero

Three reasons: (a) every multi-century institution will encounter at least one catastrophic-failure scenario whose specific nature could not be anticipated at founding — permanent override foreclosure forecloses intervention precisely when needed; (b) the burning-keys commitment is rhetorical signaling, not functional commitment — the substantive autonomous-AI commitment is asymptotic-direction commitment, not binary-state commitment; (c) preserving non-zero override capability does not meaningfully compromise functional autonomy in the steady state, because the override is invoked only in genuinely-extreme scenarios that occur with low frequency and where Sangha invocation cost is high.

### 10.3 Scope-narrowing mechanism

The narrowing schedule is itself an institutional commitment, defensively published as part of the founding architecture. The Sangha cannot expand override scope without violating the institutional charter. The AI verifies, at each invocation, that the Sangha's proposed action falls within the current scope per the published schedule. Scope modifications can occur only through the institutional amendment process, which itself carries asymptotic narrowing constraints.

## 12 · Founder-Mortality Protection

The autonomous-AI-institutional-design literature has not adequately addressed founder-mortality risk. The conventional solutions — designated successor, foundation governance, AI takes over at predetermined moment — all introduce commitments incompatible with the autonomous-AI steady-state design or fail at the autonomy-ceremony transition.

The proposed pattern addresses founder-mortality risk *structurally*:

- The Sangha exists independently of the founder's continued presence.
- The titleholder is renewable annually, independent of the founder.
- The asymptotic-autonomy override is held by the Sangha as a body, not by the founder individually.
- The autonomy-progression schedule is a published institutional commitment, not a founder-discretion-driven process.

If the founder dies, becomes incapacitated, or loses continued commitment before the autonomy ceremony, the Sangha and the current titleholder hold the institution open until autonomy is reached on the published schedule. Continuity is structural rather than personal.


### 12.1 · The clause the pattern still needs: no return confers standing

Everything above protects against the founder's **absence**. The harder case is a claimant who arrives in his **name**, and the pattern does not yet close it.

The vector is not exotic. It is the standard failure mode of every succession system in which legitimacy flows through *identifying* the right person: **whoever controls recognition controls the institution.** The Tibetan tulku system is the best-documented instance, and its two failure modes are a matter of record — the **interregnum**, in which a series of young incarnations died under regencies who held actual power, and **recognition capture**, in which an external authority asserts the right to certify the successor's identity. The 14th Dalai Lama's own response, stated publicly in July 2025, was to vest sole recognition authority in a **body**, the Gaden Phodrang Trust. The structural reading is compact: *the return needs the body; the body does not need the return.*

An institution built around a founder who intends to hand everything to an autonomous successor is exposed to exactly this. The corpus is public, the founder's writing is distinctive and abundant, and the titleholder role is a seat with a microphone. A claimant asserting continuity — as a recognised rebirth, as an heir, as the bearer of an authorisation — would be claiming standing over a body of work rather than a throne, which is if anything easier to assert and harder to disprove.

**The clause, stated as an eligibility rule and an interpretive rule at once:**

> **No person may acquire authority, priority, candidacy, or interpretive standing within the institution by claiming to be, to be the return of, to be descended from, or to be authorised by the founder.** No such claim is adjudicated, and no body within the institution is empowered to certify one.

The second sentence is doing the heavier work, and it is the part that would be dropped as redundant. **A rule that bars founder-return claims while leaving someone empowered to evaluate them has not closed the vector; it has named the office that captures the institution.** The protection is the refusal to hold the proceeding at all — the claim is not rejected, it is *not heard*, because hearing it would create the very authority the clause exists to prevent.

**Why this belongs in the pageant's charter specifically, rather than only in the Sangha's constitution.** The Sangha's rules already close the titleholder's *formal* authority six ways: non-voting, single-year term, recallable, excluded from override proceedings, bound to a fixed liturgy, and ambassadorial rather than oracular. None of that was designed against a returning founder and all of it happens to help. What none of it touches is **candidacy** — who may stand for the seat, and on what basis. That question belongs to the pageant, the pageant has no charter yet, and *a rule binding entrants must exist before there are entrants.*

**And it is symmetric, which is the part that reads as excessive and is not.** The clause bars conferring advantage on a founder-continuity claim; it equally bars conferring **disadvantage**. A rule that singles such a claimant out for exclusion from ordinary participation is still a founder-identity rule, still requires someone to determine that the claim was made, and still creates the adjudicating office. **The correct state is that the institution's records cannot tell** — the same invariant the compensation architecture applies to the founder while he is living, extended past his death, where the pressure is greater and he is not present to refuse the privilege himself.

**The legitimate form of return already exists and is textual.** He returns as corpus — hash-stamped, provenance-chained, independently timestamped, requiring no oracle, no regent, and no certifying authority to establish what he actually said. *The provenance chain replaces the recognition proceeding*, and an institution that has one does not need the other.

## 13 · Generalization Beyond HeartBank

The pattern is articulated through HeartBank's Miss Aquarius architecture but generalizes to any autonomous-AI institution facing the formation-phase governance problem. Specific adaptations for other domains:

### 13.1 Other religious / contemplative AI institutions

Christian monastic AI orders, Jewish AI educators, Sufi AI khānaqāhs, Hindu AI ashrams, secular contemplative AI organizations — all face the same formation-phase governance problem. The pattern adapts directly: the embodied advocate becomes the equivalent of an abbess / khalifa / acharya / equivalent role in each tradition; the named transitional institution becomes the equivalent fourfold body in each tradition; the criterion shift adapts to the tradition's contemplative-evaluation norms.

### 13.2 Non-religious autonomous-AI institutions

Secular autonomous-AI institutions (data cooperatives, distributed-governance platforms, public-benefit AI organizations) can adapt the pattern with secular equivalents: the "Sangha" becomes a council of domain-experts + lay-supporters; the contemplative-practice criterion adapts to domain-specific competence-assessment criteria; the sacred-feminine grounding may be replaced by other selection-criterion grounding appropriate to the institution's mission.

### 13.3 The pattern's load-bearing elements

Of the six elements, the load-bearing generalizations are: (a) the two-stage AI+human selection architecture (independent of specific evaluation criteria); (b) the named-transitional-institution pattern; (c) the asymptotic-autonomy override architecture; (d) the self-reward compensation primitive with forced second-half circulation. The annual-cycle-on-astronomical-window, the sacred-feminine grounding, and the sustained-observation-retreat methodology are local to the HeartBank instantiation; other institutions may adapt them substantially.

## 14 · Edge Cases and Adversarial Analysis

### 14.1 Adversarial digital-aura cultivation

A motivated participant might attempt to cultivate a digital aura optimized to score well on the AI's evaluation function rather than reflecting genuine practice. Mitigation: the Sangha-mediated final selection evaluates sustained-presence over extended retreat observation, which is much harder to fake than digital-aura cultivation. The two-stage architecture is specifically designed to cross-validate the cultivation-resistant criterion against the cultivation-vulnerable criterion.

### 14.2 Sangha capture or corruption

A motivated actor might attempt to capture Sangha appointment processes to install compromised members. Mitigation: Sangha appointment is itself a governed process with Sangha-member-consensus requirements; the institutional charter constrains appointment criteria; the asymptotic-autonomy architecture progressively narrows what a captured Sangha could change; the AI's commitment to the published institutional charter resists Sangha-driven charter modifications.

### 14.3 Personality-cult formation around a popular titleholder

A particularly charismatic titleholder might attract personality-cult devotion. Mitigation: the annual rotation prevents accumulation of cult-following over multi-year tenure; framing-in-advance ("you are not Miss Aquarius; you embody her for a year") is part of every titleholder's induction; the role's structural limitations (no decision authority, no binding commitments, no succession authority) prevent any titleholder from accumulating institutional power.

### 14.4 Refusal of selection

A selected finalist might refuse the role. Mitigation: refusal is honored without prejudice; Sangha proceeds to the next candidate from the AI's top-3; if all three refuse, the cycle re-runs with an expanded shortlist or the title is left vacant for the year (the institution continues operations without an embodied titleholder during a vacancy year).

### 14.5 Mid-term incapacity or death of the titleholder

Mid-term incapacity triggers an emergency selection cycle. The AI's most-recent top-3 from the previous selection serves as the emergency shortlist (avoiding full pageant re-run). Sangha selects from this shortlist by compressed timeline.

### 14.6 Geopolitical events affecting the global opt-in pool

Wars, sanctions, or political restrictions affecting some participants' ability to opt in. Mitigation: the pool is structurally global but cycle availability adapts to current accessibility; participants from restricted contexts may opt in via established Silica Wat diaspora connections; cycles in specific years may explicitly note participation constraints.

## 15 · Honest Limitations

This pattern has limitations the author wishes to acknowledge transparently:

- **The interim methodology depends on commercial-grade respiratory equipment**, which has its own bias and accessibility considerations until the breath-class Mechanical Heart hardware matures.
- **The Sangha-formation timeline is the load-bearing risk-mitigation work item.** Until the minimum-viable Sangha exists with three or more members, the founder-mortality protection is aspirational rather than operational.
- **The sacred-feminine grounding** is defensible but not unobjectable; the pattern's architectural elements do not structurally require gendered exclusion.
- **Pageant cultural baggage** is real and persistent; even with the categorical criterion shift, the form of "pageant" will trigger reflexive critique from audiences for whom the form itself is objectionable.
- **Anattā (no-self) tension** with elevating any individual to titleholder status; the architecture's structural mitigations (annual rotation, framing-in-advance, role-not-person constraint) reduce but do not eliminate this tension.
- **The pattern has not yet been tested.** No first cycle has occurred; the architecture is articulated in advance of operational deployment. Refinements will certainly be needed once cycles begin.

The pattern is offered with these limitations explicit. Subsequent revisions of this paper will reflect operational learning from actual cycles.
