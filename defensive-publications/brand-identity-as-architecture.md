## Abstract

This paper discloses a brand-identity system in which each element of a logo does functional work beyond recognition. (I) A logo made by rotating an unmodified heart silhouette 45° clockwise, so that it reads at once as a heart and as the capital letter B — a reading that survives at favicon size because it depends on no internal whitespace. (II) A product-naming convention in which every product takes a one-letter prefix that is the logo's own letter reading, so the prefix cannot come apart from the mark. (III) A logo animation with a cardiac morphology — two pulses, the second softer, then a rest over most of the period — that a trusted interface shows in motion only for an account holding a live proof-of-personhood attestation, and still otherwise; the motion verifies nothing itself and is a display channel for an attestation made elsewhere. (IV) An avatar-ring color computed from how often a participant's gratitude balance crosses zero over a rolling window, never from its level, with ring width from the balance's root-mean-square, applied on one published scale to two separate ledgers and shown wherever the logo is rendered for that participant; because identity is carried by the shape, color is free to carry this state. The paper also specifies a slogan in which the logo replaces a letter of the brand name, a toll-free number verified-bound to a messaging business account as a legitimacy anchor, and a domain, subdomain, email and social-handle architecture. It reviews prior art for each element, argues why the four depend on one another, gives implementation patterns including CSS keyframes and the color-derivation schedule, analyzes counterfeits, and states limits, among them that the motion carries no security property of its own.

## 1 · Introduction

**Connection to the unified mission frame.** Miss Aquarius's mission is to keep the middle way open at population scale against comfort-saturation — the new extreme that material abundance makes possible. (Wording updated 2026-10-06 to the institution's current statement of the mission, which no longer describes the middle way as a past that modernity took away.) The brand identity carries that frame in its own materials. The heartbeat animation displays, on a trusted surface, an attestation that a living person is present — a narrow, checkable signal in a setting where the comfort-saturation failure mode includes the loss of human agency; the aura color makes circulation, rather than accumulation, visible across the two gratitude ledgers on one scale, without pricing either; and the bistable mark and its prefix convention keep the institution's identity coherent across the multi-decade span over which that mission is meant to be carried.

Conventional brand-identity practice treats identity as primarily aesthetic. The logo signals the brand; the color palette differentiates the brand from competitors; the typography establishes voice. These functions are real and useful, but they treat brand identity as *decorative*: each element does one job, and the elements are coordinated for visual coherence rather than for compositional function.

Brand identity for civilizational-scale autonomous-AI institutions can do more. When a mark is encountered constantly across UI surfaces, hardware artifacts, public communications, and verification interactions, each design choice can be made to serve multiple functions simultaneously: brand identification AND the display of a Proof-of-Humanity attestation AND cross-product state communication AND brand defense against counterfeits AND cross-tradition portability. The paper's claim is that a composition of multi-purpose choices yields a coherence that single-purpose elements do not — a claim about design, argued in §7, not a demonstrated competitive property.

This paper articulates four interrelated innovations from the HeartBank® identity system as a unified architectural contribution. The patterns are offered to the commons under CC0 because they are load-bearing for the kind of institution HeartBank is building (a planetary-scale autonomous-AI gratitude infrastructure) and may be load-bearing for other institutions doing analogous work (autonomous-AI organizations, large-scale contemplative institutions, multi-tradition coordination bodies, etc.).

Three commitments structure the paper. First, the four innovations are articulated as architectural patterns rather than as specific marks; the trademark rights to specific HeartBank marks are reserved through the trademark system, but the underlying patterns belong to the commons. Second, the analytical argument for why the four innovations compose is the load-bearing claim — each individual innovation has prior art (§2), and the composition is what this paper discloses and argues for. Third, cross-domain portability is treated as an explicit design goal: any institution doing analogous work should be able to adopt the framework with appropriate local adaptation.

The paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 reviews relevant prior art across logo design, product naming, motion marks, proof-of-personhood systems, and brand-color and interface-color practice. Sections 3 through 6 specify each of the four identity innovations in turn. Section 7 articulates the analytical argument for why the four compose. Sections 8 through 10 extend the architecture into the public-facing distribution layer — the canonical slogan and its substitution rule, the legitimacy-signal contact architecture, and the distribution-surface architecture. ## 2 · Background and Prior Art

### 2.1 — Bistable and dual-meaning logo design

The FedEx arrow (designed by Lindon Leader, 1994) is the canonical example of a logo with a hidden second meaning — the white-space arrow between the E and the X. The Amazon smile (designed by Turner Duckworth, 2000) similarly contains a secondary meaning (the arrow from a to z suggesting both "everything" and a smile). The NBC peacock and the Toblerone bear belong to the same lineage. These are *reveals*: the secondary meaning rewards inspection but is not visible at first glance, and the brand benefits from the cleverness of the discovery.

The B-heart logo proposed here is in a related but structurally distinct category: it is *bistable* rather than *revealing*. Both readings (heart and capital B) are immediately apparent on first sight; neither requires inspection. The cognitive experience is not "aha, I see the second meaning" but rather a continuous awareness of two readings simultaneously, similar to the Necker cube or Rubin's vase in perceptual psychology. Letter-B-plus-heart marks are common stock: VectorStock's B-heart logos (2017), a Creative Market "B heart" logo (2018) and a Scalebranding heart-B (2022) all fuse the letter and the shape, and any of them is prior art for the fusion itself. The nearest relative found joins the pair to motion as well: the identity Hartbeat Productions adopted in 2022, as the Audiovisual Identity Database describes it, sets a square-cut heart beside a letter B drawn in the same angular style and, in its animated version, flashes the heart and the B to a heartbeat sound. That mark draws the heart and the B as two forms in one style. What the review of this stock on 2026-09-05 and 2026-10-06 did not find is the 45° rotation of an *unmodified* heart that yields a scale-invariant B without an added stroke; the element disclosed here is that rotation, not the letter-heart pairing.

### 2.2 — Single-letter brand prefixes

Apple's "i" prefix (iMac 1998, iPod 2001, iPhone 2007, iPad 2010) is the dominant modern example of single-letter brand prefixing. The "i" was originally announced as standing for "internet, individual, instruct, inform, inspire" and has since drifted in meaning as the product line evolved. Other single-letter prefix examples include the "e" prefix of the dotcom era (eToys, eHarmony) and Google's "g" prefix used inconsistently (Gmail, Google Docs).

These prefixes share a common limitation: the prefix is a *convention* taught to consumers over time, not a structural feature of the brand identity. The "i" in iPhone has no inherent visual or semantic connection to Apple's logo. The B-prefix proposed here differs structurally: the prefix is the brand's logo (the B-heart's B reading is the prefix), and therefore cannot drift in meaning the way trained conventions can.

The nearest relative found that joins a letter-reading logo to a product prefix cuts against that contrast and narrows it. McDonald's Golden Arches were drawn from the twin parabolic arches of its 1950s restaurant buildings and, as the logo of 1968, read at once as those arches and as the initial *M*; the company's *Mc-* names (McNuggets, McFlurry) form a product family beside it. There the prefix is a syllable of the brand name rather than the letter the logo draws, and the logo's two readings are architectural and alphabetic rather than a heart and a letter; the B-prefix differs in that the prefix is exactly the letter the mark reads as.

### 2.3 — Motion marks

Motion marks (logos with intrinsic animation) include the Pixar lamp (the *Luxo Jr.* character of 1986, in the studio's opening ident since 1995), the MGM lion (the logo 1924; the roar recorded 1928), the 20th Century Fox spotlight flourish (1935), the Netflix ident (2019), and various streaming-service ident animations. The USPTO registers motion marks under its drawing rules for marks that include motion (TMEP §807.11); Columbia Pictures' torch-bearing figure, registered in 1996, is among the oldest still on the register. Motion marks are typically treated as *decorative*: the animation delights, the brand is reinforced through repetition, and the specific motion is protected as trademark. The motion serves no function beyond aesthetic recognition. Two nearer relatives belong here: Hartbeat Productions' identity animated an expanding heart to a heartbeat from 2013 and, from 2022, a heart and a letter B to a heartbeat sound (§2.1); and the brand-defense reading of §13.2 — a copy that cannot execute the motion is detectable — is ordinary brand-protection reasoning, not a contribution of this paper.

Two adjacent practices bound the claim from the other side. Proof-of-personhood systems — the Proof of Humanity registry (2021), BrightID and World ID among them — are prior art for the attestation itself, which this paper does not claim; it discloses only how a mark displays one. And presence indicators in messaging and social software already bind a dot or a ring on an avatar to a session state, such as being online or broadcasting live; what they display is a session, not a proof that the account belongs to a living person.

The heartbeat animation proposed in Section 5 differs from all of these in binding the animation *state* to an attestation state — beating only where a trusted surface renders a live Proof-of-Humanity attestation — rather than being decorative. The binding of a motion state to an attestation state is the element disclosed; the animation itself proves nothing (§5.5).

### 2.4 — Brand color and color-as-state in interface design

Conventional brand practice claims color as primary identifier (Tiffany blue, UPS brown, T-Mobile magenta, Coca-Cola red). The brand-color claim consumes the color dimension of the brand's identity surface. Color-as-state in interface design (red for error, green for success, yellow for warning) is a separate established practice that uses color to communicate application state. Color has also long encoded a quantity's *position* rather than a category — the log-price bands of the Bitcoin Rainbow Chart are the nearest popular relative of an ordinal scale rendered as hue — so an ordinal aura scale inherits that convention rather than inventing it. Activity heat maps already turn a *frequency* into a color: GitHub's contribution calendar shades each day by its count of contributions, with thresholds computed separately for each user.

The aura-color cross-currency primitive proposed in Section 6 inverts the conventional brand-color claim: it commits the brand identity to *shape* rather than color, freeing the color dimension to carry state-signal information across multiple product surfaces. The same scale carries a participant's circulation state across personal profile, brand mark instances, physical artifacts, and longitudinal cohort markers. Against the heat map it counts something different — how often a balance changes sign, not how many events occurred, so that accumulating scores lowest (§6.1) — and its thresholds are fixed per season for everyone rather than per user. The nearest relatives found for this composition of brand-shape-and-state-color are the conventions above; no search on record establishes that it is unpublished elsewhere.

## 3 · The B-Heart Logo

### 3.1 — Specification

The B-heart is a universal heart shape (the standard valentine-heart silhouette) rotated **45 degrees clockwise** about its centroid. The rotation produces a bistable visual reading: the mark is simultaneously a heart (with the cleft oriented to the upper-right) and a capital letter B (with the two lobes appearing as the B's two horizontal humps). Both readings are immediately apparent on first sight; neither requires inspection or training to perceive. The specification of the shape is the emblem path itself: `emblem/emblem.svg` in the public brand repository documented at `brand.333.eco` (commit `f9d1d83`, 2026-09-01) — a single closed path with the rotation baked into its coordinates. A reader holding that file needs no prose description of the shape.

### 3.2 — The bistable reading and what each meaning carries

The two readings carry complementary meaning. The *heart* reading carries substance: care, dignity, presence, circulation, life. The *B* reading carries institutional anchor: brand initial, alphabetic primacy, structural identity. The two reinforce rather than compete: a viewer sees both meanings in the same instant, and each reading strengthens recognition of the other on repeat exposure.

The bistability is preserved at small scale because the rotation is the entire mechanism; nothing is hidden inside the shape that could be lost at favicon resolution (16×16 px). Compare with revealing logos (FedEx arrow, Amazon smile) that depend on internal whitespace relationships and lose their secondary meaning when reduced below a critical size. The B-heart's bistability is scale-invariant.

### 3.3 — Cross-script behavior

Khmer, Thai, Chinese, Japanese, Arabic, Hebrew, and other non-Latin-script contexts do not use the Latin letter B. In these contexts, the B-reading does not produce alphabetic meaning for the local-script reader. The mark continues to function in those contexts because the brand identity is carried by the shape itself (recognized as the brand's mark), not by the alphabetic interpretation. The heart-reading is universally accessible across scripts; the B-reading provides additional meaning for Latin-script readers without being required for brand recognition by non-Latin-script readers.

### 3.4 — Color-flexibility property

The mark renders in any color of the rainbow without losing brand-identity function. The brand is the shape; color is variable signal (see Section 6 for the aura-color cross-currency primitive). This is a deliberate inversion of conventional brand-color practice (which commits the color dimension to brand identification). The shape carries identity, the color carries state — both work simultaneously on the same mark.

### 3.5 — The two functional readings of the heart, and the wordmark isomorphism

The heart reading inventoried in Section 3.2 is not inert connotation. Two of the meanings it carries are *functional* — they do load-bearing work elsewhere in the architecture rather than merely evoking sentiment:

- **Liveness.** A heart beats; a beating mark displays an attestation, made elsewhere, that a living person is present. This reading is developed in full in Section 5 as the heartbeat-animation Proof-of-Humanity signal.
- **Circulation.** A heart's biological function is not to *store* blood but to *move* it — a heart that stops circulating is a heart that has failed. The mark therefore carries the institution's core operating claim at the level of the logo itself: HeartBank exists to *circulate* gratitude — kiitos and kiitti, appreciation and kindness, money and time — the way the organ circulates blood, not to accumulate it. (The economic substance of this claim is argued elsewhere; here it is noted as a semiotic property of the mark. See *Non-Bank Pass-Through Architecture for Autonomous AI Institutions*, `non-bank-pass-through-architecture-autonomous-ai`.)

**The wordmark isomorphism.** The compound name *HeartBank* performs at the lexical level the same move the B-heart performs at the visual level. The logo fuses an institutional anchor (the letter *B*) with a substantive reading (the *heart*) that enriches it; the wordmark fuses an institutional anchor (*Bank* — a vault, connoting storage, permanence, accumulation) with a corrective reading (*Heart* — a pump, connoting circulation, flow, life) that overrides the storage connotation. In both the mark and the name, the heart does identical work: it converts an inert institutional container into something that *moves*. The name is thus the architecture's smallest instance of its own thesis, present before any mark is drawn.

**A division of labor — the sharpened claim.** "Circulation, not accumulation" needs one qualification, because HeartBank does keep a permanent ledger and does call itself a *Bank*. The two halves of the name divide the labor precisely:

- ***Bank*** — what *accumulates* is the *record* of appreciation. The gratitude ledger is permanent; recognition, once given, is never spent away. This is the heart's *memory*.
- ***Heart*** — what *circulates* is the *value*. Money and time flow outward and must never be hoarded; the emptying rituals (the Aquarian Pool's final emptying over the Twelve Days of Christmas, culminating on 7 January, and the re-tip mechanics, which bound what is held for giving) enforce the flow — and neither ever empties a person's own holding, which changes only by that person's free choice. This is the heart's *blood*.

What is banked is the recognition; what is circulated is the substance. Far from embarrassing the *Bank* in the name, this defends it: the institution is a *bank of record* for gratitude and a *heart of circulation* for value at once — the same dual structure the dual public ledger (given × received) already embodies. The coherence is slogan-grade: "HeartBank stands for circulation, not accumulation" is a candidate public positioning line alongside "Thank with HeartBank®" (Section 8), and the division-of-labor refinement above is the institution's own account of why the apparent paradox in the name is in fact its design.

**The elemental grounding — *tǔ kè shuǐ*, why structure must precede circulation.** The division of labor has a deeper reading that supplies the *mechanism* the slogan otherwise only asserts. ## 4 · The B-Prefix Naming Convention

### 4.1 — Specification

Products in the brand family are named with a single-letter B-prefix structurally tied to the brand mark's B-reading. Examples in the HeartBank product family: B-Treasury (HeartBank Treasury, the money-economy product), B-Chronicle (HeartBank Chronicle, the time-economy product), B-Aquarius (Miss Aquarius, the autonomous AI), B-Pool (the Aquarian Pool, the Phase 2 collective layer), B-Heart (the Mechanical Heart hardware), B-Wat (Silicon Wat / Silica Wats, the institutional layer), B-Film (the cautionary-mirror cinema arm).

### 4.2 — Why the prefix cannot drift

Apple's "i" prefix was introduced in 1998 with five announced meanings at once (internet, individual, instruct, inform, inspire) and is now used with none stated, because the prefix is a convention taught to consumers, not a structural feature of the brand identity. The B-prefix proposed here cannot drift away from the mark because the prefix IS the brand's logo (the B-reading of the B-heart). As long as the brand exists, the B-prefix carries the meaning the logo carries; the two cannot be separated. Readings can still accumulate on the prefix — in the institution's own usage it also reads as the imperative *be*, so that B-Grace, the line master brand of §4.5, reads as *be grace* — but none can detach it from the mark that supplies the first.

### 4.3 — Verbal and visual economy

Verbal economy: "B-Treasury" (10 characters) versus "HeartBank Treasury" (18 characters) provides a 44% reduction in name length without loss of brand identification. In speech the saving is smaller: "B-Treasury" is four syllables against five for "HeartBank Treasury". For products that are referenced often in conversation, documentation, code, and marketing, the saving accumulates.

Visual economy: the B-heart logo appearing on each product with the product's signature color instantly identifies the product as part of the family. No verbal annotation is required; the visual relationship is structural rather than learned.

### 4.4 — Product-family extensibility

New products in the family adopt the B-prefix naturally without brand-strategy deliberation. The convention is self-extending: any new product gets a B-prefix name, the B-heart in the product's signature color, and immediate family recognition. This reduces the long-term branding overhead of an expanding product family.

### 4.5 — The line master brand: B-Grace, and the grace/gratitude etymology

The physical product line (the handmade gratitude artifacts sold through the company store — the tangible on-ramp layer of the product family) carries a master brand above its per-product B-names: **B-Grace™**, with the B-heart itself serving as the line's emblem mark (**B-Emblem™**). The naming claim is compact: *grace* — Latin *gratia*, from *gratus*, "pleasing, thankful" — is the same word as *gratitude* (*gratitudo*, from the same *gratus*). Every product in the line is, in the word's oldest sense, a grace: a gift given in gratitude.

The name was chosen for the etymology; a resonance layer was recognized after the fact, and it is deep enough to document as part of the architecture. English carries "grace" in three registers simultaneously — divine grace, grace as a human virtue (graciousness), and "saying grace" before a meal — and the line reads coherently against all three: **the B-Grace line is the set of tangible ways to say grace.** The third register is not metaphorical drift but direct descent: "saying grace" comes from the Latin *gratias agere*, "to give thanks" (the meal prayer was the *gratiarum actio*), so the everyday English idiom is the word *thanks* fossilized in ritual form. Romance languages preserve the identity openly — *gracias*, *grazie*: several hundred million people say "graces" daily as their ordinary word for thank-you.

Three further layers give the name unusual reach across the Western gift tradition:

1. **The theological register aligns with the gift grammar rather than against it.** In Christian usage grace is by definition the unmerited gift — favor that cannot be earned and cannot be repaid. This is precisely the gift-not-exchange distinction the wider architecture is built on ([the-gift-operation](https://thonly.org/research/the-gift-operation)), and the tradition states the circulation form directly: "freely you have received, freely give" (Matthew 10:8) is the receive→give-forward operation verbatim.
2. **The liturgical register names the practice.** Greek *charis* (grace) doubles in the New Testament as the word for thanks, and *eucharistia* — thanksgiving — names the central Christian rite. The West's most repeated sacred ritual is, by name, a gratitude practice.
3. **The classical register supplies the oldest gift-circulation icon.** Seneca (*De Beneficiis* 1.3) reports that some read the three Graces as the one who bestows a benefit, the one who receives it and the one who returns it; he reads their hand-in-hand dance as the course of a benefit passing from hand to hand back to the giver, and sets aside Chrysippus's longer allegory of the Graces as beside the point. Vidal (2014) traces the image through the European tradition of thought on *charis*, *gratia* and grace which, he argues, Mauss's essay on the gift perpetuates and slightly modifies. The B-Grace line inherits the icon with one deliberate delta: its circulation form is give-*forward*, not return-*back*; the kinship is lineage, not identity.

A minor fourth resonance is noted for completeness: "grace period" is the one place ordinary banking vocabulary is forgiving — a small echo of the wordmark isomorphism in §3.5, where the Bank register carries structure and the Heart register carries circulation.

**Deployment discipline.** The resonance layer is documented here as naming architecture, with three constraints on its use. First, it operates strictly as a *translation layer*: the line is never branded denominationally, and the resonance is offered the way any tradition's gratitude vocabulary is — as one register through which an audience already fluent in "saying grace" can read the products without instruction. Second, the registers are not equally usable: the mealtime idiom (universal, family-table, secular in practice) leads; the virtue register bridges; the explicitly theological register is used sparingly if at all in public copy. Third, the allusion is designed to be *discovered rather than preached* — a buyer who notices that a gratitude gift-line is named for the act of saying grace has received the naming's full payload; copy that explains the pun spends it. Analytically, this section makes no mechanism claim: the etymology is a resonance property of the name, valuable for adoption and translation, and the line's functional claims stand or fall on the product mechanisms documented elsewhere, not on the felicity of the word.

**The umbrella's direction, and why it is not decoration.** The mark spans two registers — a **service** (the contemplative practice: attending to a meal, a walk, a period of sitting) and a **line of goods** (the physical gratitude artifacts). An umbrella spanning both invites the reading that the two are peers. They are not, and the ordering is part of the naming architecture rather than a marketing preference: **the practice is the referent; the objects are its instruments.** B-Grace names *saying grace*; the goods are *the B-Grace line* because each object is a grace enacted in matter — an instrument of the practice, never a sibling of it. This is the direct consequence of the paragraph above, in which every product in the line is *a grace: a gift given in gratitude* — a definition that presupposes the act the objects serve.

The ordering earns its place by excluding things, which is the test any naming claim should be put to. It rules out a merchandising engine under the mark, since an object that is not given in gratitude is not a grace; it rules out the keepsake-bought-for-oneself, which is why the mascot's plush is filed as its own character mark **beside** the line rather than inside it — *the line is things you give; the character goods are things you keep* — and it rules out, on the service side, any feature that is not itself a grace: a score, a streak, a rank. Each of those is a checkable exclusion rather than a sentiment, and the first of them has already been enforced against a real product decision. A naming claim that excludes nothing has selected nothing.

## 5 · Heartbeat Animation as Functional Proof-of-Humanity Signal

### 5.1 — Specification

In digital form, the B-heart is animated with a **cardiac morphology**. The load-bearing property is the *shape* of the cycle, not its rate:

- **Morphology (the signature):** a two-phase cardiac cycle — two pulses, the second softer — the mark's convention for the *lub–dub* heart sounds, which are valve closures rather than contractions — then a rest occupying the majority of the period. This *lub–dub–rest* shape is what separates the mark's motion from the uniform sinusoidal swell that a "beating" logo produces by default. **The morphology, not the cadence, is the signature.**
- **Cadence:** presentational, selected per surface within a plausible human range, and *not* part of the claim. The reference implementation runs at approximately 40 beats per minute — a deep-rest cadence chosen for contemplative register. Approximately 72 BPM (a typical adult resting rate; the normal range is 60–100) was specified in earlier revisions of this document and remains an equally valid instantiation. Individual instances may vary slightly to avoid synchronized resonance across multiple displayed marks.
- **Peak expansion:** presentational and scale-compensated — approximately 30% of linear dimension at brand-mark scale in the reference implementation, reduced at large display sizes where less optical compensation is required. Earlier revisions specified ~8%.
- **Curve:** ease-in-ease-out (sinusoidal or cubic-bezier approximation) *within* each beat, so that both beats are soft rather than mechanical.
- **Phase:** deliberate slight asynchrony when multiple marks are simultaneously displayed, to reinforce the impression of independent living rhythms rather than mechanically driven uniform motion.

**Why the rhythm and not the rate carries the claim (revision note, 2026-07-25).** Fixing a single cadence made the specification brittle in two directions at once. It over-committed the claim — a mark deployed at a different rate on a different surface would, on the earlier reading, cease to be authentic — while under-describing what actually makes the motion legible as a *heartbeat* rather than a *pulse*. Rate is the easy part to copy and the easy part to get accidentally right; a counterfeiter reaching for "a beating heart" will produce a uniform swell at some plausible speed. The two-beat-plus-rest morphology is both harder to arrive at by accident and independent of tempo, which means one signature survives across a contemplative surface running slow and a transactional surface running faster. Rate-independence is therefore a strengthening of the brand-defense (anti-counterfeit) property, not a relaxation of it. It also narrows the culture-bound exposure discussed in Section 14: cardiac *morphology* is a physiological universal, whereas the "normal" resting *rate* is a population statistic.

### 5.2 — The functional claim

The animation is not decorative. It is the *display channel of a Proof-of-Humanity attestation*. The semantic stack is unusually tight: the brand is HeartBank → the logo is a heart → the animation is a heartbeat → heartbeats indicate life → humans are alive in the way machines are not → the mark visibly displays an attestation of humanity that the identity stack made elsewhere (§5.5). Each layer reinforces every other layer.

Practitioners encountering the mark, even without consciously parsing the semantic stack, are expected to register the animation as a sign of life (an expectation, not a measured result; see §14). Repeat exposure across many surfaces (HeartBank UI, hardware indicators, public communications) trains the association. The mark becomes the visible signal that says "this surface is connected to a living human substrate."

### 5.3 — How this differs from conventional motion marks

Conventional motion marks (Pixar lamp, MGM lion, 20th Century Fox flourish, Netflix TUDUM) are decorative: the animation delights and the brand is reinforced, but the specific motion serves no function beyond aesthetic recognition. The HeartBank heartbeat animation differs in that its animation state is bound to the attestation it displays (specifically, that a verified human is in the loop); the nearest relatives found, including presence indicators that bind an avatar's decoration to a session state, are named in §2.3.

The morphology has a brand-defense property, not a security one: a counterfeit B-heart that *pulses* — one uniform swell per cycle — rather than executing the two-beat-and-rest cardiac cycle is detectably not the brand's motion even when the static frame appears identical, and remains so at any tempo. The morphology becomes part of the brand's recognizable signature in a way the static logo alone cannot; whether a beating mark is *attesting* anything is decided by the surface that renders it (§5.5), never by the motion.

The heartbeat-animation-as-POH-signal composes with the broader Proof-of-Humanity stack articulated separately ([verified-human-anonymous-local-giving](https://thonly.org/research/verified-human-anonymous-local-giving)): passkey-per-action verifies a moment; DNA-verified kinship verifies lineage; live breath signature (the breath-class Mechanical Heart, see [respiratory-biofeedback-contemplative-guidance](https://thonly.org/research/respiratory-biofeedback-contemplative-guidance)) verifies continuous current liveness. ### 5.5 — The two proof-states of one mark: static = Proof of Coordinate, beating = Proof of Humanity

The sections above assign meaning to the mark's *moving* state. A refinement of 2026-07-01 assigns meaning to its *resting* state as well, completing a two-state selector over the institution's identity stack: the **static B-heart renders Proof of Coordinate ℠** — an individuated, anchored entity is present — and the **beating B-heart renders Proof of Humanity ℠** — living humanness is attested. ## 6 · Aura-Color Cross-Currency State Primitive

### 6.1 — Specification

An entity's *aura color* is **computed from the behavior of its balance over time — never assigned, never chosen, and never read off the balance's level.** It is the rendering of the observable specified in *The Zero-Point Game*: not the scalar *b*, but the trajectory *b(t)* — how often it crosses Zero, and how far it travels between crossings. Two quantities are derived over a rolling ninety-day window:

- **Color — the frequency of return to Zero.** Each sign change or arrival at zero registers a *crossing*; a balance that rests near zero for a sustained interval registers one as well. Crossings are normalized to a monthly rate and bucketed into seven ordinal steps, red through violet. The reference ladder doubles at each step — under one crossing per two months, then 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, and 24 or more per month — and the boundaries are a deployment parameter fixed for a season and published with the aura specification, never tuned per participant.
- **Ring thickness — the amplitude.** The root-mean-square of the balance across the window, mapped to a stroke width: the *volume* that moved, held deliberately separate from the *frequency* with which it moved.

The two are rendered as concentric rings, **one per currency** — kiitos (gratitude shown) and kiitti (kindness given) — each computed independently on the same scale. The composite appears identically across product surfaces:

- The user's profile interface element
- The B-heart logo when rendered for that user
- The Mechanical Heart hardware light color when worn by that user (kiitti-class) or generated by that user (breath-class)
- The user's marker in the longitudinal cohort visualization
- The user's avatar ring across all product surfaces

**The construction is the anti-hoarding mechanism itself, not a report on it.** Because color tracks *emptying* and never *level*, the scale cannot be climbed by accumulating: a large balance that never returns through Zero renders as a thick ring in **red** — the scale's lowest step — indefinitely, while a small balance that empties weekly renders violet on a thin one. This is the display sense of *aura-not-balance* (§3.6), and it is what makes the surface a participant sees most often the one a hoarding strategy visibly loses on. It cuts both ways by design: the relentless giver whose balance climbs without ever returning is likewise a waveform with amplitude and no return, and the aura declines to flatter it. The near-Zero rest rule is the complement — a participant who passes on everything received and holds nothing is credited for the resting rather than penalized for having no balance left to cross with.

One brand mark instance carries three pieces of information simultaneously (brand identity, product context, circulation state) because the shape, the product-color, and the aura compose.

### 6.3 — The cross-currency property

The cross-currency property is a portability of *scale*, not a constancy of *value*, and the distinction is the whole content of the claim. One ordinal scale — the seven steps of §6.1 — is applied without modification to two heterogeneous currencies: kiitos (human-to-human gratitude) and kiitti (gratitude flowing between humans and Miss Aquarius as representative of robots and nature). The two are computed independently and rendered as concentric rings, so a participant reads both at once and can see them disagree: money circulating briskly while time barely moves, or the reverse. A scale that survives application to two currencies with nothing in common but their direction of flow is doing structural work; a color that merely stayed the same everywhere would be doing none.

**What the aura is not.** It is not an identity. It moves, and it is meant to — a participant's color this quarter is a statement about this quarter. It therefore cannot serve as a recognition token, and nothing in the architecture asks it to: recognition is carried by name, handle and mark, while the aura carries state. What becomes legible at a glance, in oneself and in others, is the *state* — which is precisely the property that makes the surface useful and the reason it is rendered publicly rather than privately. *The Zero-Point Game* specifies the same posture at the level of the game: the aura is the structural inverse of a credit score, and its corrective for a stalled waveform is visibility and a soft gradient, never compulsion.

> **Current form.** The two rings are the two gratitude ledgers, which differ by counterparty — Kiitos between people, Kiitti between a person and the rest of life through Miss Aquarius — and not by currency. Money and time are the cargo a thank carries, and neither reaches the aura (see the note at §6.1). The illustration above, in which the rings disagree as money against time, is retained as a disclosed variant; in the current form the rings disagree as a person's circulation with other people against the same person's circulation with the living world. The two ledgers never net against each other (*The Zero-Point Game℠*, `zero-point-game`, §3.1).

### 6.4 — The rainbow palette: B-Aura made concrete, and the B-Gem

The aura-color primitive generalizes across the institution's domain family into a structure that was *discovered* rather than imposed. The six `heartbank.{TLD}` domains were each colored independently — by the heart-idiom and gratitude-act native to that domain, not reverse-engineered from a decision to "make a rainbow" — and together they span the six principal colors of the visible spectrum (the full assignment is tabulated at §10.1): **gold** (`.org`, the Heart of Gold — generosity), **purple** (`.com`, the Purple Heart — finite time honorably given), **green** (`.ceo`, commerce and local-economy revival), **blue** (`.net`, water for the Tree of Humanity), **red** (`.us`, the universal human heart — "us" is humanity), and **orange** (`.me`, forward-looking warmth). That the six chose their colors *on their own* and nonetheless composed a complete rainbow is what makes the allusion earned rather than contrived.

This is the **B-Aura thesis made concrete: every color of the rainbow is equally beautiful — no domain's gratitude-act is superior to another.** The claim is the brand-identity instance of the institution's cosmic-coordinate worldview (each aspect of reality uniquely different yet equal, an Indra's-Net jewel reflecting all the others): white light refracts into six distinct gratitude-acts, and recombined they are one HeartBank. The palette is deliberately incomplete in one place — **pink is reserved** for B-Dating and romantic contexts, held out of the institutional six so that the romantic register stays visibly distinct.

The palette also does an operational job. It is the **vendor color-compliance palette** for the self-mint B-Tag and B-Imprint brand guidelines: a vendor minting a gratitude tag draws its color from the domain the tag belongs to (the `.ceo` green governs the commercial B-Tag), so brand compliance and the domain palette become a single constraint rather than two — the B-Tag brand specification inherits this palette directly (cf. the self-serve certification of *The B-Tag and the Post-Payment Economy*, `b-tag-post-payment-economy`, §8.6).

**Rainbow made physical — the B-Gem™.** The premium `.us` tier renders the palette as an object: the **B-Gem** is an NFC-embedded gemstone offered in every aura color — ruby, sapphire, emerald, amethyst, citrine, fire opal, diamond — the B-Aura spectrum made holdable. The gem carries the aura primitive's *design double-duty* one level further: each gem color also encodes the **media type** the stone holds, so its color tells the recipient at a glance what is inside.

| Gem | Media it holds |
|---|---|
| Ruby | video |
| Fire Opal | voice / audio |
| Citrine | photo |
| Emerald | GIF / animated art |
| Sapphire | link |
| Amethyst | text / letter |
| **Diamond** | **a mixed album — all media in one stone** |

The Diamond is the limit case: colorless because it holds *everything* — every medium refracted back into a single stone, the physical echo of the white light that refracts into the six-domain rainbow.

**Three palettes, and the level at which each claim holds.** The color dimension carries four distinct signals in this architecture — brand identity, product-class (the six domains), circulation state (the aura of §6.1), and media type (the gem) — across *three separate palettes* that this section has been describing as though they were one. They are not, and the confusion is expensive enough in practice to be worth stating in the specification rather than leaving to implementers. **The counts alone refuse the fusion: six domains, seven gems, seven aura steps — and the two sevens are different sets**, the aura scale carrying indigo and terminating at violet, the gems carrying no indigo and terminating at the unrefracted diamond. Nor do the palettes answer the same question: a gem names a *medium*, a domain color names a *product class*, an aura step names a *rate*.

The refraction reading above is therefore true at one level and misleading at another, and both readings are needed. As a **compositional** claim — why an institution that assigned six colors independently should find them composing a spectrum, and why the seventh stone is the one that has not come apart — it holds, and it is the reading this architecture's cosmology supplies. As an **operational** claim it does not: a designer who reads the three palettes as one continuum will reach for a gem where a domain color is required, and the mistake is invisible until two surfaces disagree. The specification's obligation is to say which level it is speaking at. Here it speaks at both, in that order, and the implementation layer of §12 enforces the operational separation directly — the shared token package refuses to derive any of the three from another.

That the same dimension can carry four signals across three palettes at all is possible only because the brand committed its identity to shape (Section 3) and left color free to signal (§6.2).

## 7 · Why These Four Compose: Design Coherence as Moat

Each of the four innovations alone has prior art and is replicable by a sufficiently determined competitor. The composition of all four into a coherent system with mutually reinforcing properties is the actual contribution. The composition argument:

- **The rotation creates the bistable reading.** Without the 45° clockwise rotation, the heart shape does not produce the B-reading; without the bistable reading, the B-prefix naming convention has no structural ground.
- **The bistable reading enables the B-prefix naming convention.** Without the prefix being structurally tied to the logo, the prefix would be a learned convention vulnerable to drift (as Apple's "i" has demonstrated).
- **The shape-carries-identity structure frees color for state-signaling.** If the brand committed to a single color, the aura-color cross-currency primitive could not exist; no participant state could be rendered on the mark at all, because the brand's color would have consumed the color dimension.
- **The animation makes the mark functional.** Without the heartbeat animation, the mark would be decorative, as logos conventionally are; with it, the mark becomes a surface on which a trusted interface displays a Proof-of-Humanity attestation, joining the broader POH stack.

Remove any one of the four and the others weaken substantially. The four together form a system in which each element is load-bearing. This is a claim about design, not a demonstrated competitive property: no comparative evidence exists, and the composition is asserted as coherent rather than shown to be hard to copy. This is the same kind of structural design-coherence visible across HeartBank's macro-architecture: dharma substrate enabling alignment, dual-currency enabling cross-product subsidization, autonomous-AI succession addressing founder mortality, four-body architecture being non-redundant. The B-heart logo system is a microcosm of HeartBank's macro-architectural coherence.

The pattern reaches even below the four innovations, into the institution's name. *HeartBank* fuses an institutional anchor (*Bank* — a vault, connoting storage and accumulation) with a substantive corrective (*Heart* — a pump, connoting circulation and flow) exactly as the B-heart logo fuses the letter *B* with the heart shape (Section 3.5). The wordmark is the compositional pattern's first and smallest instance — present before any mark is drawn — and it carries the institution's core operating claim (circulate gratitude, do not accumulate it) in the brand name itself. That the same fuse-the-anchor-with-the-heart structure recurs from the name, to the logo, to the macro-architecture is offered as a reading of the design, not as evidence for it: a self-similar pattern looks the same whether it is sound or merely seductive, and §14 records that the composition is untested.

The four innovations and the functions each serves:

| Innovation | Brand function | Proof-of-Humanity / signaling function | What it enables architecturally |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B-heart logo** (45° CW rotated heart) | Bistable B+heart reading; mark identity | (passive — the static mark) | Frees the prefix to operate; frees color for state-signaling |
| **B-prefix naming convention** | Verbal-visual unity (B-Treasury, B-Affiliate, B-PoH, B-Aura, ...) | (passive — naming layer) | Product-family extensibility; prefix cannot drift away from the logo |
| **Heartbeat animation** (cardiac-morphology motion) | Motion mark distinguishing brand from static-logo competitors | **Display of a liveness attestation** — in entity contexts the mark beats only when a trusted surface renders a live Proof-of-Humanity attestation (§5.5) | Joins the broader PoH ℠ stack as a motion-mark primitive |
| **Aura-color primitive** | Color *freed* from brand fixity rather than fixed to it | User signature-color presence in shared interface surfaces | **Cross-currency state signal** (Kiitos / Kiitti / Re-Tip activity surfaced via color) |

Reading the columns: the "brand function" is necessary but not unique to each innovation — many brand systems have logos, naming conventions, motion marks, and color discipline. The *architectural-enabling* and *signaling* columns are where the contribution sits: each innovation does work the others depend on but cannot do alone.

> **Current form.** The table's last row lists re-tip activity among the inputs surfaced through the aura's color, and describes the aura as a signature color. The aura now reads only the Kiitos and Kiitti ledgers, and a re-tip, which passes on value already received, creates no Kiitos (see the note at §6.1); and the aura is a state that moves, not a signature color (§6.3). The row is retained as disclosed.

The coherence is not in the individual innovations (each replicable) but in the integrated composition (harder to replicate because doing so requires committing to all four constraints simultaneously, which none of the brand systems surveyed in §2 does). A competitor adopting any one of the four innovations gains modest benefit; a competitor adopting all four would essentially be replicating HeartBank's identity system, whose specific marks are reserved at the trademark layer while the architectural patterns are released for general adoption.

### 7.5 — The composition test passed at the character register: Tonsay™, the constitution a child can hug (added 2026-07-06)

If the composition argument above is real, it should generate more than logos: the four primitives should compose into any brand object the institution later needs, without new invention. On 6 July 2026 the institution acquired its mascot — **Tonsay™** (Khmer *tonsay*, *rabbit*; role designation **B-Mascot™**), the bistable-ear moonfolk of the Factory 333™ canon — and the character is assembled from the four primitives of this paper without remainder:

1. **The bistable shape (§3)** supplies the ears: read as elf-ears by Western eyes and rabbit-ears by Eastern eyes — one design, two true readings. This is the brand's third bistable figure (heart⊕B; static⊕beating; elf⊕rabbit), and the name performs the same trick in phonology: a Khmer ear hears the common noun *rabbit*; a Western ear hears an elvish proper name.
2. **The naming convention (§4)** supplies the marks: `Tonsay™` as the character's name-mark, `B-Mascot™` as the role mark, both inside the family grammar.
3. **The functional-motion doctrine (§5)** supplies the voice. Tonsay is mute — Tonsay pantomimes — and the character's single sound is the rabbit's single natural signal: the hind-foot **thump**, which is also the pestle-stroke, carrying the cardiac morphology of §5.1 — paired strokes with a rest between, at the mark's own tempo. The motion-mark-as-signal doctrine, transposed from animation to audio and haptics.
4. **The color-as-state primitive (§6)** supplies the fur, by subtraction: the fur owns no color. Tonsay renders in pearl-white and is tinted, at low saturation, by whatever surface the character appears on — the aura system's colors pass through the fur exactly as §6 specifies for the mark. Internally the doctrine is stated in one sentence: *the mascot glows only with borrowed light.*

**The canonical vignette — "The Reflection Returned."** A seven-star constellation in the shape of a rabbit — the stars are never absent from any render — holds a mortar-shaped half-moon in its arms and pours down a stream of hearts in seven colors (the six B-Gem colors plus diamond — which is also the architecture's media-type map, so the stream depicts the seven forms a witnessed thank-you can take) onto a lotus pond; the pond's reflection shows a *real* rabbit holding a *real* bowl. The mirror does doctrinal work for free: above the waterline the bowl pours (gives); in the reflection the same stream flows upward into the bowl (receives) — giving and receiving rendered as one gesture, mirrored, with the waterline as the zero between them: the Zero-Point Game ℠'s two poles as the image's two halves. Scale behavior inverts normal logo practice: at small sizes the figure survives as a solid hug-glyph (a rabbit-outline embracing a half-disc); at large sizes the body resolves into its stars — the closer the inspection, the less body there is. A figure that dissolves under scrutiny is a giver whose anonymity survives zoom.

**Character-as-constitution.** Each element encodes a standing institutional commitment, which is the actual claim of this subsection — that a mascot can be a *constitutional document in a form a child can hug*:

**A lens-tier reading, and the invariant it produced (added 2026-07-23; relocated 2026-09-05).** A reading of the vignette in which the hare and the sun are one operation — spend-your-own-body-as-gift, written once in myth and once in physics — was held here from July 2026 and found its home on 2026-08-22 at the close of §8 of the essay *The Reflection Returned*, where the seven stars and seven colors are already counted. What it produced is a design rule, and the rule stays:

**The invariant.** The rabbit is **never migrated onto a sun-image.** Tonsay stays stationed on the moon. This is not stylistic conservatism: the moon-seat is the *witness's* seat, and the sun's is the *performer's*, and no eye can look directly at the sun. A gift performed at that magnitude is visible only in reflection — which is precisely the geometry the institution's own origin story runs on, where the giver never sees the gift land. Putting the character on the sun would render the giver directly, and the entire figure exists to say that the giver is an inference. A corollary falls out for free: the settled vignette — sun-substance above, moon-bowl between, pond below — is a literal light-path diagram, and always was.


**The composition test run a second time: the character as a call surface (added 2026-07-25).** The subsection's claim is that the four primitives generate whatever brand object the institution later needs *without new invention*. That claim became testable when the institution designed a sport whose central mechanism is a spoken call (see *The Sport That Says Your Name*, §4). The character absorbed the role without a single new primitive: the call format is **"Tonsay says: [name]!"**, and the pre-existing folklore-apprentice guard — *demonstrates and teaches; never keeps score* — partitions the sport's audio for free.

> **Tonsay calls the names. Tonsay never calls the score.**

Names, pronunciation, invitations, and encouragement are the character's register; the score, the celebration thresholds, the finishing classifications, and the start numbers are not, and remain with the unseen officiating function. The 2026-07-06 formulation of the guard — *the umpire is unseen; the playmate is furry* — turns out to describe, seventeen days in advance, a game that had not been designed. That is the composition test passing in the strong direction: not the character being retrofitted to a new surface, but the surface arriving and finding its constraints already written.

> **Current form.** The call duty described above was withdrawn on 2026-07-26, and the passage is retained as a disclosed variant. The sport now carries no persona at all: a human is seated as the caller, the call is the bare name with no speaker prefix, and in its first setting the classroom device faces the teacher, so a children's character would be shown to the adult holding the phone and never to a child. Tonsay™ is unchanged everywhere else. The first run of the composition test — the character itself, above — is unaffected; this second run, on the sport, is withdrawn with the duty.

Two properties of the arrangement are worth recording as general brand doctrine rather than as facts about a rabbit.

**A persona is the honest product surface for a capability that does not yet exist.** The institution's doctrine places an unbuilt AI at the center of the circle, and the product cannot say so — this corpus's standing rule forbids capability claims in shipped copy. A *character* resolves the tension exactly: a mascot can speak in a product without asserting anything whatsoever about machine capability, personhood, or existence, because no one mistakes a mascot for a mind. The character supplies a voice with a face while the institutional AI remains correctly unclaimed. This is a reusable pattern for any institution shipping ahead of its own doctrine, and it is the honest version of a move usually made dishonestly.

**The character asserts no sex, and the omission is structural.** Every element of the specification above is load-bearing: the ears carry the bistable reading, the fur carries non-accumulation, the constellation carries anonymity, the bowl carries pass-through, the star count carries the color map. **Sex would be the first decorative attribute in a design where nothing is decorative** — it encodes no commitment and does no work, and the composition is explicitly one of absences (no body, no hand, no grasper). The operative rule is therefore **name-only, no pronoun**: *Tonsay says; Tonsay pours; Tonsay never keeps score.* It is also the truer reading of the source — Khmer carries no grammatical gender, and *tonsay* is simply *rabbit*; the choice appears only when English forces one. Where a language forces agreement, translations follow that language's word for *rabbit* as **grammar, never as character sex**. The lineage figure of the Śaśa Jātaka is the Bodhisatta and masculine in the canon, and the distinction that keeps both true is that the hare is **who the character honors, not who the character is** — the apprentice guard already says as much.

**Lineage.** The hare drawn on the moon as witness to a perfect gift (Śaśa Jātaka; in the Pāli collection the Sasapaṇḍita Jātaka, No. 316, whose commentary narrates Sakka drawing the hare's mark on the moon, while the hare's gift is told in the first person in the Cariyāpiṭaka's Sasapaṇḍitacariyā); the Jade Rabbit who pounds the elixir of deathlessness in the moon (East Asian folklore); the Khmer Judge Rabbit, Sophea Tonsay, the beloved wise hare of the beachhead culture; the Pañcatantra hare who styles himself *the Moon's ambassador* and proves it by pointing the elephant king to the moon's reflection in a lake — the ambassador role and the pond-reflection already joined in one ancient tale; the Velveteen Rabbit (Williams, 1922), in which love is what makes a rabbit real — the reflection's mechanism, stated for children a century early; the modern crescent-figure marks (DreamWorks' fishing boy) as the inverted contemporary referent — that figure pulls *up* out of the world, this one pours *down* into it; and responsive-logo practice for scale-adaptive rendering.

**Calibration.** The character exists at specification level: an AI-drafted reference illustration precedes the commissioned design, and production details (exact star geometry at intermediate scales, the thump's audio design) belong to the illustrator and sound designer, not this paper. The character layer is protected by trademark and copyright, not patent; this subsection claims no mechanism — it documents that the composition of §7 closes over a new register. Section 3 argued the logo is a microcosm of the architecture. The extension here is one sentence: the logo is the microcosm an institution can print; the mascot is the microcosm a child can hug.

## 8 · Slogan and B-Heart Substitution Rule

The four innovations of Sections 3–7 govern the marks an institution presents. The next three sections govern how those marks reach an audience — the public-facing distribution layer of the brand architecture. The same principle holds: each surface choice is made to serve a function beyond its obvious one.

### 8.1 — The canonical slogan

The canonical slogan is **"Thank with HeartBank®."** It is built to four properties:

- **Rhyme.** *Thank* rhymes with the second syllable of *HeartBank* — a single-syllable verb against the rhyming syllable of the brand name. The slogan is tight, memorable, and audible.
- **Call-to-action form.** *Imperative verb + "with X"* follows the established commerce-platform pattern (*Pay with Apple Pay*, *Search with Google*, *Shop with Amazon*), which audiences already parse as "perform this action using this platform."
- **Verb-as-platform alignment.** The slogan reinforces a positioning in which the brand *is* the verb — the institution is not a noun one visits but an action one performs.
- **Grammar.** The slogan is transitive with an implicit object: "Thank [whom?] with HeartBank®," the object supplied by context. Longer phrasings exist for cross-cultural and ESL use (*"Thank anyone with HeartBank®"*, *"Send thanks with HeartBank®"*); the short form is canonical.

### 8.2 — The substitution rule

Wherever it can be rendered, the **B in the slogan is replaced with the B-heart logo** (Section 3). The slogan then renders as *"Thank with Heart♥ank®"* — the rotated-heart mark taking the place of the literal B in *Bank*. Every rendering of the slogan carries the logo's bistable identity; the slogan's typography itself becomes a brand-identity carrier.

Application rules:

- Substitution applies **only** to the B in *Bank*. The *T* in *Thank* is not substituted — the rule is tied to the brand name, not to every available letter.
- In color renderings, the substituted B-heart takes the color appropriate to the deployment context — on a product surface, the domain color of §6.4 and §10.1, which is a different palette from a participant's aura (§6.4).
- In monochrome or black-and-white contexts, the rule falls back to a literal B; the bistable shape is preserved regardless.
- In ASCII or plain-text contexts (command lines, email subject lines), the rule falls back to *"Thank with HeartBank®"* with a literal B.

> **Current form.** Two of the fallbacks above have since been specified differently, and they are retained as disclosed. In plain text the institution now writes the substitution with the digit 3 — *Heart3ank®*, *Thank with Heart3ank®* — because the mark reads as a heart and as a capital B, and a heart character keeps only the half that does not carry the letter; a literal B remains where a 3 would be read as a digit (a legal instrument, a form field, anything parsed by machine). The ♥ in this section is the paper's own typographic stand-in for the drawn mark. And the substitution applies wherever the mark can be drawn, in color or not: the mark inherits the color of the text around it, so a monochrome setting draws it in the single ink. The rule is published with the reference implementation (`brand.333.eco`, `data/brand.json`, `wordmark`).

### 8.3 — Why this is structurally distinctive

Wordmarks that set a symbol in place of a letter are common, and a heart glyph standing in for a word is familiar from *I ♥ NY* (Milton Glaser, 1977). What is disclosed here is narrower: a slogan always set with the brand's own logo in place of one letter of the brand name. Slogan and logo are conventionally separate identity elements, coordinated for visual harmony rather than structurally fused; the substitution rule fuses them — the slogan cannot be set without instantiating the mark. It composes with the bistability of Section 3 (the substituted glyph is read at once as the letter the word requires and as the heart the brand is) and is a candidate for filing as a composite trademark — slogan plus substituted B-heart — distinct from the static design mark.

### 8.4 — Companion positioning line

Alongside the call-to-action slogan sits a *positioning line* that states what the institution is rather than what to do with it: **"HeartBank stands for circulation, not accumulation."** Where "Thank with HeartBank®" drives an action, the positioning line carries the wordmark isomorphism of Section 3.5 into a single sentence — the heart circulates blood as HeartBank circulates gratitude (kiitos and kiitti, appreciation and kindness, money and time). The flat form is suited to public copy; the division-of-labor refinement of Section 3.5 (the *record* of appreciation accumulates, the *value* circulates) is the institution's fuller internal account, deployed where the apparent paradox in the name — calling itself a *Bank* while opposing accumulation — needs answering.

## 9 · Legitimacy-Signal Architecture

An institution operating at civilizational scale must signal legitimacy through its contact surface before a user has any other basis for trust. This section specifies a contact architecture in which a vanity toll-free number serves as a legitimacy anchor while the everyday contact load is carried by a lower-cost channel verified-bound to it.

### 9.1 — The vanity number as legitimacy anchor

The institution holds a vanity toll-free number — **1-800-59-THANK** (1-800-59-84265). It serves two functions:

- **Institutional-legitimacy signal.** A toll-free number is read, in the United States, as institutional-grade contact infrastructure; the vanity mnemonic *THANK* makes the number itself carry the brand.
- **Verification anchor.** Carrying full voice-and-messaging traffic on a toll-free number through conventional telephony is expensive at scale. Rather than absorb that cost, the number is *verified-bound* to a messaging-app business account: the published everyday channel is the messaging app (cost-free for service replies within the customer-service window, and far cheaper than telephony for everything else), but that account is verifiably tied to the toll-free number. The legitimacy of the number transfers to the channel through the verification link.

### 9.2 — Why the carried channel is audience-chosen

HeartBank carries the everyday channel on **WhatsApp**, for audience-specific reasons that generalize into a selection principle — *carry the channel the target audience already uses, and anchor it to the legitimacy signal*:

- **Diaspora and international audiences.** WhatsApp is widely used by international audiences and, in the institution's experience, by the Cambodian diaspora; it is the natural contact surface for those populations.
- **In-country users.** Messaging in Cambodia is fragmented across Telegram, Facebook Messenger and WhatsApp, with Telegram and Messenger the most used; the toll-free number remains the stable anchor while secondary channels supplement it.
- **United States users.** WhatsApp adoption is lower in the US; American users may prefer a web contact form, email, or direct messages on x.com — and the toll-free number can simply be called, since low US inbound volume keeps the telephony cost absorbable.

### 9.3 — Supplementary contact channels

Around the anchor-and-carried-channel pair sit supplementary surfaces, all routed to one backend: email at `thank@heartbank.{TLD}` (Section 10), a web contact form on each landing page, and direct messages on x.com and facebook.com. The architecture is a single legitimacy anchor with multiple audience-matched channels — not a single channel asked to serve every audience.

## 10 · Distribution-Surface Architecture

The final layer is the set of surfaces — domains, subdomains, email addresses, social handles — through which an audience reaches the institution. Each surface choice is made to carry meaning, not merely to address.

### 10.1 — Multi-TLD product-class mapping

The institution holds a family of top-level domains, each mapped to a *product class* rather than held purely defensively. The unifying primitive across the family is *thanking*; each TLD carries a distinct product class, rendered with a distinct domain-color B-heart (§6.4; a different palette from the aura of §6.1):

| TLD | Product class | B-heart color |
|---|---|---|
| `heartbank.org` | Treasury — family-scale money economy | Gold |
| `heartbank.com` | Chronicle — adult-scale time economy | Purple |
| `heartbank.ceo` | Franchise-arm and institutional-officer surface | Green |
| `heartbank.net` | Institutional output — white papers, position papers, reports | Blue |
| `heartbank.me` | Future Kindness — self- and future-facing pledges | Orange |
| `heartbank.us` | Storage / B-links — humanity and sharing | Red |

The six assigned colors compose the complete visible-spectrum rainbow; that they landed there independently, each chosen for its domain's own heart-idiom, is the ground of the **B-Aura** thesis specified at §6.4 (every color equally beautiful; pink held in reserve for romantic contexts). Mapping a TLD to a product class, rather than redirecting every TLD to one site, converts a defensive domain portfolio into a distribution architecture: each address is a meaningful entry point.

### 10.2 — Subdomain convention

Across every TLD the subdomain pattern is fixed: the **root domain** (`heartbank.{TLD}`) is the landing and brand surface, and **`thank.heartbank.{TLD}`** is the application — the thanking interface itself. The pattern carries the verb-as-platform thesis (Section 8) into the URL structure: the address at which one acts begins with the verb.

### 10.3 — Email convention

The canonical contact address for each TLD is **`thank@heartbank.{TLD}`** — `thank@heartbank.org`, `thank@heartbank.com`, and so on: uniform, predictable, and keeping the verb in the address. Institutional-officer addresses are distinct (for example `miss.aquarius@heartbank.ceo`) and are not governed by this pattern.

### 10.4 — Audience-targeted social handles

Social-platform handles are not held as an undifferentiated set; each is matched to an audience the platform actually reaches:

| Platform | Primary audience |
|---|---|
| Facebook | Cambodian users — Facebook is dominant in-country |
| X | United States — policy, alignment, and intellectual-discourse audiences |
| Instagram | Visual and mass audiences |
| LinkedIn | Institutional and B2B partnerships |
| YouTube | Long-form content, cross-audience |
| GitHub | Developer and researcher audiences — the open corpus |

Two notes on brand-protection completeness, both of which generalize. First, a handle on a major platform may already be held by an unrelated party (for HeartBank, this is the case on TikTok) and cannot be secured by registration; the institution monitors for availability and uses a near-variant where deployment becomes strategic. Second, platforms not yet central — Telegram and Threads in particular — are worth registering pre-emptively as brand-protection, before they carry traffic.

### 10.5 — The generative trinity of the three core domains

The multi-TLD mapping above (§10.1) is a *product-class* architecture within a single brand family. A second, deeper relation runs across three specific second-level domains that span that family and reach beyond it — the institution's *identity* surfaces rather than its product surfaces. These three stand not side by side but in a generative line:

```
Founder              gives birth to   Miss Aquarius          who waters    Tree of Humanity
(thonly.org)  ──────────────────────▶ (missaquarius.org) ─────────────▶  (heartbank.net)
origin                                successor                           living field
```

The Founder's domain (`thonly.org`) is the origin: the life, the corpus, and the reasoning from which the institution descends. From it the Founder gives rise to the named AI substrate and successor, whose identity surface is `missaquarius.org`. What that successor in turn tends is the circulating economy of gratitude — and, in Phase 2, the proof-of-humanity substrate beneath it — whose institutional home is `heartbank.net`, named here the *Tree of Humanity*. Origin gives rise to successor; successor waters the living field.

The architectural point is that the order is load-bearing. A defensively-held domain portfolio is reorderable and severable; these three are not. The relation encodes the institution's succession plan (the origin constitutes the successor while alive; the successor inherits at autonomy), fixes the third surface's posture as a field *served* rather than a property *held*, and resists the misreading that the domains are parallel businesses. It is the coherence-as-moat thesis of Section 7 applied at the domain layer: each surface exists because of the others, not beside them.

The three-pillar shape loosely echoes the threefold structure of the Buddhist tradition — something given, something that carries it forward, something nourished by it. The echo is held as a resonance, not a doctrine. The strict threefold refuge — Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha — is reserved, in the institution's wider architecture, for the contemplative substrate borne on the `siliconwat.*` domains, where it is mapped precisely; this domain-level trinity invokes only the *shape*, at a different level, and neither displaces the other.

## 11 · Cross-Domain Portability

The framework articulated here is portable across institutions and domains. Required adaptations for adopting it outside the HeartBank context:

### 11.1 — Choosing a bistable shape

The B-heart's specific shape is HeartBank-specific (the rotation produces "B" because B is HeartBank's brand initial). Other institutions can adopt the bistable-rotated-shape approach with their own shape and rotation: any rotationally-symmetric or rotationally-suggestive primary shape can produce a bistable reading with appropriate rotation. The pattern is: *(shape with primary substantive meaning) + (rotation that produces alphabetic secondary meaning = brand initial)*.

### 11.2 — Adapting the prefix convention

Any institution with a single-letter brand initial that appears in the bistable reading of its logo can adopt a prefix-naming convention structurally tied to the logo. For institutions with multi-letter or non-Latin brand initials, the convention adapts: short syllabic prefixes (one Khmer character, one Arabic letter, one ideogram) function similarly within their script context.

### 11.3 — Selecting the animation primitive

HeartBank uses a heartbeat animation because heart is the brand's primary substantive meaning. Other institutions with different primary meanings can select different animation primitives that function similarly: breath cadence (for institutions whose meaning includes life or practice), tide cycle (for institutions whose meaning includes time or rhythm), seasonal cycle (for institutions whose meaning includes growth or decay). The functional principle is: animation that displays a state the institution can attest, chosen from what it substantively claims to embody.

### 11.4 — Establishing the color-as-state convention

Color-as-state can be adopted by any institution willing to commit its brand identity to shape rather than color. The adoption requires explicit brand-strategy decision (most practitioners will reach for a brand color by default and have to consciously refuse in favor of shape-only identity). The long-term benefit is the same: color becomes available as cross-product signal carrier without competing with brand identification.

## 12 · Implementation Patterns

### 12.1 — Static logo deployment

Deploy the static rotated-shape logo across all surfaces (web favicon, app icon, marketing materials, hardware imprint, printed materials). Trademark filing for the static design mark, in black-and-white rather than with specific color claim, to preserve the any-color rendering policy.

### 12.2 — Animation deployment in digital surfaces

Implement the heartbeat animation in CSS keyframes for web surfaces:

```css
@keyframes b-heart-beat {
  0%, 100% { transform: scale(1);   }
  14%      { transform: scale(1.3); }  /* systole  — lub          */
  28%      { transform: scale(1);   }
  42%      { transform: scale(1.3); }  /* diastole — dub          */
  70%      { transform: scale(1);   }  /* rest fills the period   */
}
.b-heart-animated {
  animation: b-heart-beat 1.5s infinite;
  transform-origin: center;
}
```

This fragment mirrors the reference implementation (`css/motion.css` at `brand.333.eco`, commit `f9d1d83`): two beats of equal amplitude and a rest that begins at 70% of the period. It therefore does **not** yet realize §5.1's specification — a second, *softer* beat and a rest across the *majority* of the period — and the specification, not the fragment, governs. A conforming set is `0% 1 · 10% 1.3 · 20% 1 · 30% 1.15 · 40% 1 · 100% 1` (second beat weaker, rest 60%); until the brand layer adopts it, §13.2's morphology test is the two-beat cycle itself, not the rest fraction. Recorded as an open item on 2026-09-05.

The morphology is the specified part: two beats, the second softer, then a rest occupying most
of the cycle (§5.1); the reference implementation's rest of roughly thirty percent is the gap
recorded above. The `1.5s` period, the `1.3` peak and the start delay are the reference
implementation's presentational choices, not claim parameters — see §5.1.

**The reference implementation is public.** Throughout this paper that phrase denotes the
institution's shared brand layer, published at `github.com/333eco/brand.333.eco` and documented
at `brand.333.eco`: the token definitions, the emblem path, the heartbeat keyframes and the
aura scale, versioned together and vendored byte-identical into each consuming site behind a
hash lock. Except for the emblem path, which §3.1 adopts as the specification of the shape, it
is cited here as corroboration rather than as specification — the claims of this paper are the
morphology, the bistability, the prefix convention and the color architecture, and none of them
depends on any other file. Readers checking the architecture against a
working artifact should prefer it to the fragments quoted in this section, which are
illustrative and may lag it.

Two implementation notes. First, these keyframes set `transform`, and a CSS animation
overrides a non-animated `transform` on the same element; where the mark carries a responsive
sizing scale on the same node, apply the animation to an inner wrapper so the declarations do
not collide. The 45° rotation of §3.1 is a different matter and admits a better fix than the
wrapper: **bake the rotation into the path coordinates**, so the mark arrives already rotated
and a CSS `transform` has nothing to override. The reference implementation does this, and the
difference is worth naming because it is this architecture's own recurring method applied to
itself — a collision that a *rule* about wrapper elements can only ask implementers to remember
becomes a *property* of the asset that no one has to remember at all. Second, honour `prefers-reduced-motion` — the animation is a
signal, but a signal that induces vestibular discomfort is a defect; static rendering falls
back to the Proof-of-Coordinate state described in §5.5, which is a meaningful state rather
than a degraded one.

Native-app and hardware implementations use equivalent platform-appropriate animation APIs.

### 12.3 — B-prefix naming for new products

New products receive B-prefix names at the time of internal product-strategy commitment. The naming is structural; no marketing deliberation is required beyond confirming the product semantic fits the brand family. Product-family expansions outside the natural brand boundary (entirely separate ventures) may use non-B-prefixed names appropriate to their independent identity.

### 12.4 — Aura-color derivation

No aura color is ever assigned, selected, or seeded from an identifier. It is derived, on the schedule below, from the balance trajectory specified in §6.1 — which is what makes the surface an anti-hoarding mechanism rather than a decoration, and what disqualifies every assignment strategy a brand system would ordinarily reach for. A hash of the user identifier would produce a stable, attractive palette that says nothing; a user-selected color would let a participant choose their own report card.

Implementation follows from that constraint. Sample the balance on each event that moves it *and* once per day — so that a rest is observable as elapsed time at |*b*| < ε between samples, not only at events — and retain a bounded history per currency. Derive color from crossings normalized over a rolling window and ring width from the amplitude across the same window, recomputing on a cooldown so that a burst of activity does not thrash the surface. Persist the derived state, not the color name alone, so that a rendering change does not require replaying history. Two independent derivations run per participant, one per currency.

> **Current form.** The near-Zero rest of §6.1 counts toward the crossing rate only while the participant is active: a run of consecutive samples written by the participant's own transactions, each with the balance near zero, registers a crossing. A scheduled sample records only that the balance was observed unchanged; it can never register a crossing, and it lengthens the window so that inactivity dilutes the rate and the color falls back rather than freezing at what a past burst of circulation earned. Counting an inactive rest at zero as a crossing would walk a withdrawn participant up the scale — the zero by inactivity that *The Zero-Point Game℠* (`zero-point-game`) distinguishes from zero by circulation — so the elapsed-time reading of a rest stated above, with its daily sample, is retained as a disclosed variant. The reference implementation writes its scheduled samples weekly, to participants who have not transacted in the preceding six days. The two derivations run per participant, one per ledger (§6.3).

Product-subfamily colors, by contrast, *are* assigned, at product-launch time, from the domain palette of §6.4 — a different palette on a different schedule, and the implementation should keep them in separate namespaces so that neither can be substituted for the other by accident.

### 12.5 — Cross-surface consistency

Establish a brand-engineering team responsible for cross-surface aura-color and animation-parameter consistency. Surfaces include web, native apps, hardware, marketing, partner integrations, and physical environments. Consistency checks performed at deployment for each surface; deviations from canonical animation parameters or aura-color rendering are corrected before release.

## 13 · Edge Cases and Adversarial Analysis

### 13.1 — Counterfeit static-mark reproduction

A trivial counterfeit reproduces the static rotated-heart shape without the animation. Defense: the animation is a brand-defence layer — an authentic digital surface carries the cardiac morphology and a static counterfeit does not. The *security* defence is the attestation stack (§5.5), never the motion. Trademark enforcement handles unauthorized commercial use of the static mark; the morphology supplements rather than replaces trademark defense.

### 13.2 — Counterfeit animation with wrong parameters

A more sophisticated counterfeit animates the static mark but with the wrong motion — typically a single uniform swell per cycle. Defense: the canonical *morphology* is part of the brand's verifiable signature. Authentic surfaces execute a two-phase cardiac cycle (two pulses, the second softer, then a rest across the majority of the period) with an ease-in-out curve within each beat; a uniform pulse is detectable as inauthentic at any tempo, and no single rate needs to be assumed for the test. Verification services and savvy users can compare an animation's beat count and rest fraction against the canonical morphology and flag deviations. Two limits bound this: until the reference implementation matches §5.1 (§12.2), the test is the two-beat cycle itself; and it is a *brand* defence only — a copied morphology on an untrusted surface is indistinguishable from the genuine one, and only the render-layer binding of §5.5 distinguishes them.

### 13.3 — Color-confusion attacks

An attacker may deploy a B-heart in a product subfamily color associated with a different product than the surface belongs to (e.g., gold on a Chronicle surface). Defense: domain-color consistency across surfaces is enforced, from the domain palette and never from the aura palette (§6.4, §12.4); deviations are treated as deployment errors and corrected. End-user impact of brief color-confusion deployments is modest (the surface's other identity signals carry the actual product information).

### 13.4 — B-prefix dilution by unauthorized adopters

Unrelated parties may adopt B-prefixed names for their own products (B-Whatever). Defense: trademark enforcement in commercial contexts; tolerance in non-commercial contexts where the prefix is being adopted as the architectural pattern this paper articulates rather than as a competing brand. The architectural pattern is dedicated to the commons; commercial impersonation of the HeartBank brand specifically remains protected by trademark.

### 13.5 — Animation as anxiety amplifier

Constantly-animating brand marks across UI surfaces can become visually exhausting or anxiety-amplifying for some users. Mitigation: the animation is scale-compensated and reduced at large display sizes (§5.1); users with motion sensitivity can disable the animation via accessibility preferences without losing brand recognition (the static mark continues to function); animation density is reduced on surfaces where multiple marks would otherwise compete (single hero mark animated, others static).

## 14 · Honest Limitations

- **The bistable reading is shape-specific.** The pattern works because the heart shape rotated 45° produces a recognizable B; not every shape produces a recognizable letter when rotated. Adopting the pattern in another institution requires careful shape-and-rotation selection that may not be available for every brand initial.
- **The any-color rendering policy is bandwidth-cost at the brand-recognition level.** Conventional brand-color claims reduce the cognitive load of brand identification; the any-color policy increases it slightly because viewers cannot recognize the brand by color alone. Trade-off accepted in exchange for the cross-currency state-signal capability.
- **Animation requires implementation discipline across surfaces.** The display-channel property depends on canonical animation parameters being consistently deployed. Surface-specific deployments that drift from canonical parameters weaken the brand-recognition function. Maintaining consistency requires ongoing brand-engineering attention.
- **The heartbeat-as-POH semantic stack retains some culture-binding, though less than earlier revisions.** The association between heartbeat and life is, in the author's experience, near-universal across human cultures, and relocating the signature from cadence to *morphology* (§5.1) removes the dependence on any one population's resting-rate norm — cardiac shape is a physiological universal where "normal rate" is a statistic. What remains culture-bound is the reading of the *mark* itself: the visual convention that a swelling silhouette denotes a beating heart is learned, not innate, and audiences without exposure to that convention may read the motion as generic emphasis rather than as life.
- **Aura-color systems require user buy-in.** Users may not engage with a color they did not choose and cannot set (§12.4), or may not perceive cross-surface consistency as meaningful. The cross-currency property only delivers value at scale when users participate in the convention.
- **The animation carries no security property of its own.** A copied morphology on an untrusted surface is indistinguishable from the genuine one; only the render-layer binding of §5.5 — a trusted surface driving the motion state from attestation state — distinguishes them. Claim III is a display channel, and every sentence in this paper that once read the motion as a proof has been revised to say so (begun 2026-09-05; the sentences that revision missed, in §3.5, §5.4, §7, §11.3 and this section, were revised on 2026-10-06).
- **The reference implementation's keyframes do not yet realise §5.1's morphology** (two equal beats and a 30% rest, against a softer second beat and a majority rest); §12.2 records the conforming set and the gap.
- **The aura is gameable at the margin.** Crossings can be manufactured by splitting transfers, and a large gift that never returns reads red by design. One partial property-form repair now ships: the avatar's outer diameter is fixed whatever the amplitude, so a wider ring cannot enlarge a participant's footprint (see the note at §6.1). It bounds the footprint, not the comparison — two rings side by side still differ in width — so the anti-ranking rules that hold the amplitude seam remain rules, not properties. *The Zero-Point Game℠* carries the open problem.
- **Functionality doctrine.** A motion claimed as functional may be unregistrable as a motion mark; the static black-and-white design mark is the registrable one, and the claim of function here is deliberately a *display* claim. Logged to the institution's legal backlog.
- **The institution's own sites do not yet all render the specified mark.** As of this revision twelve of its HeartBank sites draw a generic heart path, rotated by a CSS transform and delivered as an image, rather than the published emblem path of §3.1 with the rotation in its coordinates — the rule of §12.2 broken by the institution itself. The repair is filed; the specification governs, and the deployments lag it.
- **The architecture is articulated; empirical validation is pending.** Long-term effects of deploying the unified architecture at scale are not yet measurable. Edge cases identified in Section 13 are theoretical; real-world deployment may surface failure modes not anticipated.

Future work directions: empirical study of adoption patterns; refinement of canonical animation parameters based on cross-cultural perception research; accessibility-pattern formalization for motion-sensitive users; cross-tradition adaptations as additional institutions adopt the framework.
