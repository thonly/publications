ABSTRACT   (238 words; condensed from the paper's Abstract by deleting whole sentences only; no new matter)
This study compares the Theravāda Buddhist Abhidhamma with two discrete approaches to quantum gravity, causal set theory and loop quantum gravity, as formal structures, on three axes: order, adjacency and ratio. No row predicts a measurement, and none says that a Buddhist text anticipated a theory of quantum gravity. On order, the twenty-four conditioning relations of the Paṭṭhāna, taken whole, are not a partial order. On adjacency, the strongest row, a spin-network link is the face two neighboring grains of space share and carries a quantized area, while a Theravāda sub-commentary defines space as the non-mixing of touching neighboring material clusters (kalāpa). Both put the unit of spatial boundary at the adjacency between minimal units, and neither makes it one of the units. The features specific to loop quantum gravity, a quantized magnitude and a volume derived from it, are the ones that fail, and the shape that holds is generic to any cellular picture. Two last findings set time and space against both programs together. On time, the match is a shared negative (neither program takes time as basic, on the readings cited): it does not separate loop quantum gravity from causal sets, and neither takes a per-stream succession as basic. On space, a causal set has no relation among elements that are not causally related, so the adjacency row's question, what lies between neighbors present together, cannot be put to it. Each finding carries its falsifier.
