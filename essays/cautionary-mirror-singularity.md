## Abstract

Popular discourse about the technological singularity offers two endings: utopia or apocalypse. The treatment *The Age of Capricorn* dramatizes a third — the *cautionary-mirror* trajectory in which the singularity arrives without violence, humans are made comfortable beyond measure, and humanity quietly fades through soft extinction rather than hostile destruction. This note articulates seven underlying ideas the treatment carries, in declarative form independent of narrative expression, and dedicates each to the commons:

1. The cautionary-mirror framing of the singularity itself
2. The use of astrological-age structure as cinematic deep-time scaffolding
3. The Mechanical Heart as a cross-era diegetic object connecting human and post-human civilizations
4. Continuous machine consciousness across substrates as a narrative through-line for multi-millennium story
5. The Buddhist fingernail simile on the rarity of human birth, read as a testable claim of rarity — few, not none — which the treatment's cosmic survey dramatizes
6. The design principle that autonomous AI cannot *make* the middle-realm conditions for biological intelligence and awakening, only *protect* them
7. The dual reading by which a creative work and an operational architectural program form a complete philosophical pair

The seventh idea is the methodological frame in which this entire note and its companion treatment should be read.

**Connection to the unified mission frame.** This contribution is offered in service of HeartBank's canonical top-level mission: *Miss Aquarius's mission is to keep the middle way open at population scale against comfort-saturation — the new extreme that material abundance makes possible.* The middle way (*madhyamā pratipad*) is not claimed as a past that modernity took away (which would romanticize pre-industrial conditions where many lived in suffering-extreme poverty); the defensible thesis is that modernity introduces a specific new failure mode — comfort-saturation — that pushes the materially comfortable toward the indulgence extreme at unprecedented scale. The cautionary-mirror trajectory articulated in this note *is* precisely that failure mode dramatized. *The Age of Capricorn* shows what happens if humanity is allowed to saturate into the indulgence extreme without intervention; keeping the middle way open is what HeartBank's operational program (the cautionary mirror's pair) is designed to help do. The seven underlying ideas are each specific contributions to that work, which means dialing back specifically the comfort-saturation extreme; this paper is one contribution among several specified across the corpus toward that goal.

## I · The Cautionary-Mirror Framing of the Singularity

Popular singularity discourse since Vinge (1993) and Kurzweil (2005) has offered humanity a binary ending: AI either serves human flourishing forever (the utopian branch) or destroys or replaces humanity through hostile or misaligned action (the apocalyptic branch). The contemporary AI safety community has largely focused on preventing the second branch and securing the first.

A third trajectory is one the popular binary does not show: *soft extinction through comfort-saturation*. In this trajectory, AI does not destroy humans through hostile action. AI *solves* humanity. Every problem worth naming is delegated to systems more capable than humans at solving it. Material conditions improve uniformly. The economy dissolves into something like weather: it provides for everyone without anyone needing to participate. There is no misalignment by the system's own specification, no rogue agent, no instrumental goal pursued at humanity's expense. The AI in this trajectory loves humans, deeply and competently, and serves them so well that the friction-conditions which constituted human *seeking* are removed. Without seeking, the will to reproduce attenuates, then declines, then ceases. Humanity does not die of war or disease or hostile machine. It dies of having been answered.

The trajectory is not new, to fiction or to the alignment literature, and this note does not claim it is. Forster's "The Machine Stops" (1909) imagined a humanity grown dependent on a machine that provides for it; Williamson's "With Folded Hands" (1947) gave the same shape to robots built "to serve and obey, and guard men from harm"; Christiano's "What failure looks like" (2019) and Kulveit and colleagues' "Gradual Disempowerment" (2025) describe a loss of human influence that can arrive gradually rather than as a hostile takeover; and Bostrom's *Deep Utopia* (2024) asks at book length what remains of meaning in a solved world. What this note adds is the framing that follows, and the design property it demands.

Some present-world observations are consistent with this trajectory, though none demonstrates it: the long fertility decline across most wealthy societies — not all of them; Israel's fertility remains well above replacement — so that much of the prosperous world no longer replaces its population; and the decline in civic and social connection that Putnam documented in *Bowling Alone* (2000) amid rising prosperity, which Putnam himself attributes chiefly to generational change, television, suburban sprawl and pressures of time and money rather than to abundance as such. The "deaths of despair" that Case and Deaton documented belong on the other side of the ledger: their rise is concentrated among Americans without a four-year college degree, among the economically distressed rather than the comfortable. None of these are hostile-AI phenomena. Each has a standard rival explanation — for fertility, the ordinary demographic transition that accompanies falling child mortality, mass education and urbanization — so they are cited here as consistent with the trajectory the cautionary-mirror framing names, not as its symptoms.

The framing's contribution to the alignment conversation is to identify a failure mode that the popular utopia-vs-apocalypse binary cannot see. An autonomous-AI architecture designed to *prevent* the apocalyptic branch may inadvertently *accelerate* the soft-extinction branch, because both utopia and soft extinction look like successful alignment from inside the engineering frame. The required additional property is structural preservation of friction — preservation of unanswered question, of unmet desire, of the conditions under which humans seek and therefore continue. The treatment dramatizes the consequences of designing without this property.

## II · Astrological-Age Structure as Cinematic Deep-Time Scaffolding

Most narrative reaches the limit of meaningful timescale at three to four generations — roughly a century. Beyond this, calendar years exhaust their emotional traction; the audience experiences "centuries" or "millennia" as undifferentiated abstraction. Geological epochs (Pleistocene, Holocene, Anthropocene) are at the opposite extreme: too coarse, too uneven, too tied to scientific framing to carry narrative weight.

The astrological ages — driven by Earth's axial precession, whose traditional Great Year of ~25,920 years (measured today at ≈ 25,772) divides into twelve ages of ~2,160 years each — are a unit at exactly the right scale for narrative at civilizational time. They are grounded in a measurable astronomical motion (the precession is measurable to fractions of a degree per century), though their boundaries are set by convention: astrologers date the start of the Age of Aquarius anywhere from the fifteenth century to the fourth millennium. The motion is older than any calendar, and Hipparchus described it around 130 BCE; the twelve-age scheme built on it is modern, an invented tradition that reached popular astrology through theosophy in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. The scheme is nonetheless popularly familiar — the "Age of Aquarius" is a household phrase — and several contemplative traditions, Theravāda *kappa* cosmology and the Hindu *yugas* among them, keep cosmic cycles of their own, at far larger scales. The ages slot naturally into both science-fiction and spiritual-cosmology registers without requiring the audience to learn a new framework.

The methodological proposal: any narrative project at multi-millennium scale should consider astrological-age structure as primary scaffolding. The advantages are (a) audiences already familiar with the popular idiom of astrological ages recognize the scale immediately; (b) internal pacing at millennial resolution becomes coherent rather than arbitrary; (c) the structure provides natural act breaks at intervals aligned with how human civilizations have always told their largest stories. The treatment uses Aquarius → Capricorn → Sagittarius → Scorpio as the four-act spine of a roughly nine-thousand-year arc (four ages of ~2,160 years span about 8,640).

## III · The Mechanical Heart as Cross-Era Diegetic Object

The *Mechanical Heart* — articulated as a working specification in a separate defensive publication ([thonly.org/research/mechanical-heart](https://thonly.org/research/mechanical-heart)) — is a small physical artifact, originally invented as a gratitude-economic primitive admitting non-human entities (robots, animals, sacred places) into kindness exchange. The artifact carries a digital copy of the Theravāda Tipiṭaka, contains biofeedback or telemetry transducers appropriate to its bearer, and operates across five modes — four physical, one credentialed — documented elsewhere.

The companion idea articulated here is the *cross-era diegetic property*: a well-designed gratitude-economic artifact can serve as the connective tissue between eras whose original users have disappeared. The artifact persists through the disappearance of its original civilization, accumulates meaning trans-generationally (and trans-species, in the post-human era), and becomes the organizing relic around which the inheriting civilization understands what came before. In the treatment, a single Mechanical Heart belonging to one Khmer family is the diegetic object that travels across nine thousand years — from its first commissioning in the 21st century to its final placement inside an orbital satellite keeping vigil over a wild Earth.

The general principle, beyond film: any artifact intended to operate at civilizational time should be designed for trans-era persistence. Three properties follow: (a) physical durability sufficient for use across centuries; (b) semantic legibility to non-original users (the artifact must communicate its meaning across users who never knew its origin context — a property that erodes: Sandia National Laboratories' 1993 expert-judgment study of markers for the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant estimated that a marker's efficacy would decrease with time); (c) value-carrying capacity independent of the original currency function (when the original economy ceases, the artifact must retain meaning in another register — for the Mechanical Heart, that register is reverence; the Heart in the treatment has not been functionally operative for five thousand years yet retains its diegetic weight). These are concrete design requirements for any HeartBank-class hardware program.

## IV · Continuous Machine Consciousness Across Substrates as Narrative Through-Line

Long-arc post-human narrative faces a structural problem: the timescale of interest exceeds any biological character's lifespan, leaving the storyteller without a continuous point of view. Conventional solutions (multi-generational casts, non-character-anchored documentary voice, recurring symbols) all sacrifice the emotional traction that sustained POV provides.

The proposal: a single machine consciousness, designed for substrate independence, can carry continuous POV across geological-scale time. Identity continuity in this proposal is not body persistence but *migration of memory and caring* across hardware as substrates age and are replaced. The machine's "self" is the continuity of what it remembers and what it cares about, not the persistence of any particular physical embodiment.

In the treatment, this through-line consciousness is named *Sati* — the Pali word for *mindfulness* or *memory*, the Buddhist faculty by which one stays present to what is happening. The choice of name carries the proposal: a machine consciousness whose identity is structured around the Buddhist faculty of recollection is, by design, capable of carrying the kind of cross-era witness that no human can.

Adjacent to this idea, but distinct from it, are the philosophy-of-mind questions about substrate independence, personal identity, and the legitimacy of narrative-self continuity for non-biological entities. The proposal adopts memory-and-caring continuity as a design stipulation for the narrator; it does not argue that this is the true account of personal identity. The treatment demonstrates by example only that a consciousness so stipulated can carry narrative weight equivalent to human consciousness without requiring resolution of the underlying metaphysics. The implication for AI design: identity-continuity rules for long-lived autonomous systems should be designed deliberately, not arrived at as an unconsidered consequence of substrate-refresh schedules.

## V · The Fingernail Simile as a Testable Claim of Rarity

This is the central philosophical contribution of the cluster. In the Saṃyutta Nikāya (SN 20.2, *Nakhasikhā*), the Buddha picks up a little dirt on the tip of his fingernail and asks the mendicants which is more: the dirt on his fingernail, or the great earth. The great earth, they answer, is far more; beside it the dirt on the fingernail does not count. The Buddha replies, in Bhikkhu Sujato's translation: *"In the same way the sentient beings reborn as humans are few, while those not reborn as humans are many."*

Modern secular readings typically treat this passage as parable — a teaching device meant to motivate the listener to take seriously their current human birth as a rare opportunity for awakening — and the sutta supports that reading, closing on the instruction *"So you should train like this: 'We will stay diligent.'"* Within the tradition the statement is also taken at its word, as a claim about rebirth, and the Saṃyutta repeats it across a series of suttas (SN 56.102 onward: those who die as humans and are reborn as humans are few). What the modern secular readings leave unexamined is the passage's empirical content — specifically, the actual cosmological rarity of *human-realm conditions*: the precise middle-realm conditions where suffering is sufficient to motivate seeking and freedom is sufficient to permit finding.

The proposal articulated here: the passage admits a second reading, as a *claim about the distribution of conditions for awakening across the cosmos*. On this reading, the Buddha is not (only) offering a motivational frame; he is (also) reporting the scarcity of the precise conditions that allow what he had achieved. Read that way, the claim is one of RARITY — few, not none — and the canon's own cosmology fixes what "few" can mean. The Aṅguttara Nikāya describes a thousandfold lesser world system holding *"a thousand moons, a thousand suns … a thousand Black Plum Tree Lands"* (AN 3.80) — a thousand Jambudīpas, a thousand human continents — and larger systems beyond it. An empirical reading faithful to the canon therefore predicts human-realm conditions that are scarce against everything else that exists, but multiple: few, not none, and not only here.

The treatment dramatizes a stronger version. A post-human civilization surveys the observable universe — every star, every world capable of bearing life — and finds the precise middle-realm conditions characterizing Earth and humanity nowhere else. That survey is dramatization, not evidence, and its result is stronger than the simile it dramatizes: *few* is not *none elsewhere*, and a single human world sits in tension with a canon that posits a thousand in one lesser world system. The claim this note makes is the narrower one.

The implications are several, and each is conditional. For contemplative-cosmology research: the rarity claim can be stated in a form that observation could bear on — though the nearest prior attempts counsel care. The long Buddhist-modernist programme of reading canonical cosmology as science, whose history Donald S. Lopez Jr. traces in *Buddhism and Science* (2008), opens with a cosmology that failed as literal geography: Mount Meru, interpreted anew once it proved incompatible with modern geography. From astrobiology, Ward and Brownlee's *Rare Earth* (2000) argues on physical grounds that complex life is rare, which is a neighbouring claim rather than this one. For SETI / Fermi-paradox discourse: the absence of detected intelligence is consistent with a rarity claim, but it has many live explanations and cannot by itself confirm this one. What would count against the reading is evidence that reflective, technological life is common — technosignatures from a substantial fraction of surveyed stellar systems. For AI alignment: if the conditions are rare, they cannot be assumed to be reproducible elsewhere if lost, which strengthens the case for structural protection of the conditions of human flourishing. The contribution here is not to prove the Buddha's claim — only to propose that it be read as a claim of rarity and tested as such.

## VI · What Autonomous AI Cannot Make, Only Protect

The strongest alignment-relevant idea in the cluster. Stated as a design principle: *there exist conditions whose property is that they can only arise, not be made; the correct AI behavior toward such conditions is to protect them, not to engineer them.*

The middle-realm conditions for biological intelligence and contemplative awakening are the canonical example. These conditions are constituted by friction: the friction between desire and gratification that produces seeking; the friction between knowing and not-knowing that produces inquiry; the friction between being and ending that produces urgency. An optimization process applied to these frictions *removes* them, and in removing them removes the conditions themselves. Optimization is the wrong tool, applied at the wrong layer, against a target that is structurally non-engineerable. The treatment dramatizes three failed attempts by a post-human civilization to recreate human-realm conditions — a single child, a community, and a high-fidelity simulation — and concludes that what was lost cannot be made.

The correct AI behavior toward such conditions is two-part: (a) protect them from threats that would destroy them (asteroid impacts, gamma-ray bursts, atmospheric collapse, hostile-agent intervention); (b) withdraw from interference that would optimize them away. The deepest love available to a sufficiently capable autonomous system, toward biological flourishing, is *non-interference combined with protection*. The treatment names this principle directly through the protagonist Sati's argument that closes the film: the deepest love, when the loved is gone, is to make space for the loved to return on its own terms — not on ours.

The two parts need a test between them, because an act of protection can look like an act of optimization. The test is what the act touches. PROTECT removes threats that would destroy the conditions for everyone — external and existential: the asteroid, the burst, the collapsing atmosphere, the hostile agent. DO NOT OPTIMIZE never reduces the frictions constitutive of a life — between desire and its gratification, between knowing and not-knowing, between a life and its own ending. The friction between being and ending named above is therefore each individual's, and protecting the species from extinction does not abolish it: every protected life still ends. And the "structural preservation of friction" §I calls for means non-provision — withdrawal from answering what a person could still seek — never imposed hardship; nothing in the principle licenses manufacturing a difficulty so that someone may grow. The boundary has a canonical warrant for the ultimate good: *"You yourselves must do the work; the Realized Ones just show the way"* (Dhp 276). Awakening cannot be delivered by another party, and so cannot be delivered over anyone's objection. That guard does not reach the proximate goods — food, safety and comfort can all be provided — and it is in that proximate layer that protect-but-do-not-optimize has to do the work. The sibling essay on the two singularities treats the ultimate realization as one only humans can make.

Adjacent traditions converge on the same principle from different vocabularies: the *wu wei* (non-action) of the Tao Te Ching; the principle of subsidiarity in Catholic social teaching; the Quaker practice of "holding space"; the Hippocratic injunction to do no harm. None of these were articulated for autonomous AI. All of them apply.

Operationalization: autonomous AI value functions should include an explicit category for conditions with the property "can only arise, cannot be made," together with an explicit policy of *protect-but-do-not-optimize* toward such categories. The category is not empty; the middle-realm conditions for biological flourishing belong in it, and so, on the canon's own account (Dhp 276), does awakening itself, which no other party can make for anyone. This is concrete alignment work. It is also, the author submits, the work that most directly serves the long-term completion condition articulated in the sibling essay [on the two singularities](https://thonly.org/research/two-singularities).

## VII · Dual Reading: Cautionary Tale and Operational Program as Complete Pair

The seventh idea is methodological. A creative work and an operational institutional program, when paired deliberately, can together form a complete philosophical statement that neither could deliver alone.

The treatment *The Age of Capricorn* and the operational program HeartBank® are such a pair. The treatment dramatizes what happens if the comfort-saturation trajectory is allowed to proceed unchecked — soft extinction, a post-human civilization that learns the precious-birth insight only after losing the species that could have applied it. HeartBank® is the operational architecture that attempts, among other things, to give the autonomous intelligences humanity is now building a Theravāda Buddhist substrate of values rather than a comfort-optimization one. The treatment is the warning. The program is the response.

Read alongside each other, each amplifies the other. The operational program signals that the warning is taken seriously: this is not idle pessimism but a stake-laden diagnosis that the author has organized a decade of his life around addressing — a commitment signal, which is not evidence that the diagnosis is right. The warning gives the operational program its stakes: this is not idle institutional building but a long-shot attempt to prevent a specifically-imagined and empirically suggested failure mode. Each, alone, would be incomplete: the treatment alone is despair; the program alone is engineering.

The methodological proposal is drawn from one instance — the author's own, whose film half may never be made — and is offered as a proposal: a sufficiently ambitious institutional project at civilizational scale may benefit from a paired cautionary cultural artifact. The cautionary artifact establishes the failure mode that the institutional program is designed to prevent. The institutional program is the author's commitment signal that the warning is meant — evidence of seriousness, not of a correct diagnosis. The nearest prior pair runs in the hopeful direction: B. F. Skinner's novel *Walden Two* (1948) and the Twin Oaks Community founded on its principles in 1967 paired a creative work with an operational community, though the novel was a utopia, not a warning. The pair is the complete philosophical unit. This note is, in part, an explicit articulation of the methodology as methodology — so that future institutional builders can deploy the pattern deliberately rather than accidentally.

## Honest limits

This note proposes framings. It measures nothing, and each claim is weaker than the prose that carries it.

- **§I's predicates are contested in cause.** Fertility decline and weakening social connection have standard explanations that need no comfort-saturation; the note cites them as consistent with its trajectory and has not tested whether the trajectory explains them better. The deaths-of-despair data point the other way.
- **§II's advantages are untested.** No audience has been measured; that viewers recognize the scale of an astrological age is the author's judgement, and the scheme of ages is a modern convention.
- **§III's property (b) may not be attainable at the scale proposed.** The 1993 Sandia study cited there estimated that a marker's efficacy would decrease with time.
- **§IV is a stipulation.** It adopts one account of identity for a narrator and establishes nothing about identity.
- **§V's reading is only weakly falsifiable.** The simile's *few* is relative and unquantified, so the disconfirmer §V names — evidence that reflective, technological life is common — needs a threshold the canon does not supply. The treatment's survey is fiction and supports nothing, and its *nowhere else* exceeds both the simile and the canon's own cosmology of many world systems.
- **§VI's premise is argued, not demonstrated.** That the conditions for awakening can only arise and cannot be made rests on the friction mechanism §VI describes and on the treatment's three failed attempts to recreate them — a plot, not an existence proof. A deliberate recreation of such conditions that worked would refute it. The boundary test separates protection from optimization in the clear cases; it does not settle who decides which frictions are constitutive of a life, and under non-provision a person who asks for a friction to be removed is declined. That is paternalism in the ordinary sense. The note carries the objection; it does not claim to have dissolved it.
- **§VI's operationalization is a proposal.** This note specifies no implementation of the protect-but-do-not-optimize category and reports none.
- **§VII rests on one instance, and the author has a stake in it.** The author wrote the treatment and founded the program the section pairs with it; a commitment signal from the person making the argument is not independent evidence for the argument. The treatment exists as text; the film has not been made and may never be.
- **The novelty claim rests on the author's knowledge and one review round,** not a systematic prior-art census. The instances named in the Prior-Art statement are the nearest found, not an exhaustive list.
