## The companion, and where it goes further

I have already written about the money version of this idea. The essay is called *Emotional Infrastructure as a Public Good*, and its claim is that anonymous, proximity-verified generosity — a tip that arrives from someone who was *here, in this room, just now*, but whom you cannot identify — restores something modern public space has lost: the felt sense of being held in another human's awareness. When the giver is unknown, the kindness stops attaching to one person and becomes a property of the whole space. The waitress is not thanked by a customer; she is thanked by the afternoon. I argued that this is a missing category of infrastructure, with the economic shape of a public good, and that the cultural form already pointing at it — the *Faith in Humanity Restored* videos, the viral pull of unexpected anonymous kindness — is a constituency waiting for the architecture it has not had.

This essay is the companion to that one, and it reaches further than I expected when I started writing it. The money version operates on the texture of public life: how strangers feel in shared space. The time version operates on something deeper and more dangerous — on *mate selection*, on the oldest sorting mechanism the species has, and through it on how men and women treat each other in ordinary life. I did not set out to write about dating. I set out to extend the anonymous-nearby primitive symmetrically from money into time, because in HeartBank's Chronicle the second currency is time, and time given between strangers is — if you look at it without flinching — essentially a date. The moment I saw that, I saw the rest of it, and I have not been able to un-see it since.

So let me state the thesis at the top, plainly, before I earn it. **When the thing that gets you a partner is re-gated on proven kindness rather than appearance and wealth, sexual selection itself is redirected toward kindness — and because the mate-drive is the root drive of the species and the money-drive is largely derived from it, re-gating the root partially heals the derivative.** This is the most powerful lever I have found anywhere in the entire project, and it is also the one most likely to be misread, misused, or to hurt someone if it is built carelessly. Both of those facts are load-bearing, and I will hold them together for the whole essay.

## The same gift, extended into time

I need to sketch the mechanism, but only enough to ground the argument — the full specification lives in the corpus, and this is an essay, not a spec.

HeartBank® has two currencies. The Treasury (money) is family-to-family. The Chronicle (time) is adult-to-adult: you give someone an hour of your presence, your attention, your company. In the Chronicle, the same primitive that carries the money version reappears in time. You give time, anonymously, to people who are nearby — verified as real human beings, physically present, but unidentified. You accumulate it slowly. And the match, when it happens, is not a swipe. It is a quieter thing: when two people have each, anonymously and independently, given enough toward the *same* shared hour, a reveal becomes *available* — not automatic. Both have to choose it. The double-blind structure means that no explicit rejection ever happens; you simply never reach the threshold, and no one is ever handed the cruelty of a "no." I call this layer **B-Dating℠**, and I want to be honest that I am slightly afraid of it, for reasons I will come to.

Four properties of this matter for the societal argument, and I will lean on each:

- **The signal is aura, not the individual thank.** What makes a person visible in this layer is not any single gift but their **B-Aura** — the aggregate, anonymous, many-sourced reputation for proven kindness built up over a long course of time, acknowledged by many nearby strangers who had nothing to gain by acknowledging it. This is a community-sourced behavioral record no dating product has ever had.
- **Underneath sits Proof of Humanity ℠.** Every participant is verified real, verified single, verified human — anonymous until mutual reveal. This natively kills the two plagues of online dating, catfishing and bots, not as a feature bolted on but as a property of the substrate.
- **The slow accumulation is the point.** Proven-over-a-long-course is, by construction, anti-hookup and pro-durable-bond. It self-selects for people capable of commitment, and it serves the large, underserved, swipe-fatigued population that wants partnership rather than the next match.
- **Kindness raises visibility, never entitlement.** No one is ever owed a match. Aura makes you more *seen*; it never earns you access to a person. I will return to this guard repeatedly, because it is the single hinge between a revolution and a dystopia.

That is enough mechanism. The interesting part is what happens to a society when this becomes one of the ordinary ways people pair off.

## The cheapest signal and the most expensive one

Consider what a swipe-based dating app actually optimizes. It optimizes the cheapest-to-fake signal in existence: a photograph. A photograph can be staged, filtered, borrowed, or generated outright. It carries almost no information about whether the person behind it is kind, honest, or capable of love. The entire apparatus of modern dating has been built on top of the one signal that is easiest to counterfeit, and then everyone is surprised that the experience feels counterfeit.

Now consider what B-Dating gates on. It gates on a long, anonymous, many-sourced trail of acknowledged kindness toward strangers who had no reason to flatter you. This is — in the precise sense the biologist Amotz Zahavi gave the term — an **honest signal**: trustworthy *because* it is expensive to fake. You cannot buy an aura. You cannot filter it. You cannot generate it in an afternoon. The only way to accumulate a long record of being kind to many nearby people over a long course of time is to *actually be kind to many nearby people over a long course of time*. The cost is the guarantee. A predator cannot counterfeit a years-long trail of locally-witnessed gratitude any more than a peacock can fake the tail it cannot afford.

Here is where it stops being a product observation and becomes a societal one. Biologists distinguish *inter*sexual selection — what one sex finds attractive in the other — from *intra*sexual selection — how members of the same sex compete with one another for access to mates. In our current arrangement, intrasexual competition is overwhelmingly a competition of *display*: of resources, of status, of the markers of provisioning capacity. An enormous fraction of human striving — the conspicuous consumption, the status games, the accumulation that has no point past sufficiency — is, underneath, intrasexual competition wearing a suit. We compete to display, because display is what the selection environment currently rewards.

Change what the selection environment rewards, and you change what the competition optimizes. If the thing that makes you visible to a potential partner is not the watch but the aura — not what you have gathered but what you have given — then the competition to be chosen becomes a competition in *prosociality*. And because the aura is built from anonymous kindness to *everyone nearby* (you cannot know in advance which of the strangers you are kind to will turn out to be the one who vouches for you, or the one you are meant for), the competition cannot be gamed by being kind only to the people who might date you. It generalizes. The instruction it writes into ordinary life is: *be good to every stranger, because the one you are meant for might be among those who vouch for you.* Everyday kindness becomes romantically load-bearing — for everyone, all the time, not as performance but as the slow accumulation of a record that cannot be faked.

```
        WHAT INTRASEXUAL COMPETITION OPTIMIZES

  Current selection environment        Re-gated on proven kindness
  ----------------------------         ---------------------------
  signal:   resource / status display  aura (acknowledged kindness)
  fakeable: cheaply (photo, watch)     no (years of witnessed giving)
  scope:    aimed at desirable targets generalized to everyone nearby
  competes  by accumulating, by        by giving, by being good to
  by:       displaying                 strangers who cannot repay you
  spillover hoarding, conspicuous      a warmer commons; kindness as
  into life: consumption, status anx.  the ambient currency of being seen
```

I want to be careful not to oversell this, and I will spend a whole section later being careful. But the structural point is sound: sexual selection is among the most powerful species-shaping forces there is, and right now we point it at display. Pointing even a portion of it at proven kindness is not a small thing.

## The drive beneath the drive

This is the part of the argument I find hardest to say without it sounding grandiose, so let me say it as plainly as I can and let the reader apply the discount.

The mate-drive is the root drive of the species. The money-drive is, to a very large degree, *derived* from it. Money is not, for most people most of the time, wanted for itself; it is wanted as a proxy in a much older tournament — for the status, the provisioning capacity, the display that the mating environment has rewarded for as long as there have been mates to win. We have built an entire civilization of accumulation on top of a derivative drive, and then we treat the accumulation as the fundamental thing and the mating tournament beneath it as a private matter. I think we have it backwards. The accumulation is the symptom; the selection environment is the cause.

Every other engine in HeartBank motivates kindness *against* the current. The self-thank, the manufactured occasions for giving, the patron flywheel — all of them are trying to get a self-interested creature to behave generously, and all of them are, in a sense, swimming upstream against the pull of self-interest. Mate selection is different. Mate selection does not fight the current. Mate selection *is* the current. It is the single strongest motivator the species has, and right now it is pointed, through the money-drive, at accumulation and display. If kindness becomes a path to mating, then the most powerful motivator in the entire system is redirected onto the exact behavior the institution exists to cultivate — and it is redirected not by exhortation but by the oldest incentive there is.

And here is the move that made me sit still for a while when I first saw it. If you re-gate the root drive on kindness, you do not only redirect the root. You partially *detoxify the derivative*. If proven kindness — not displayed wealth — becomes a real path to being chosen, then some of the desperate, anxious, zero-sum quality of the money tournament loses its fuel, because the tournament was always partly a proxy war for the mating tournament underneath. You heal the thing underneath the thing. I do not claim this is total, or fast, or guaranteed. I claim the causal arrow exists and that almost no one points it the right way. Re-gate the root; the derivative loosens its grip.

## Sexual selection redirected toward kindness

Let me place this in the larger frame I have been building, because it is not a stray idea — it completes a structure.

I have argued elsewhere, in *Two Singularities*, that the AI age has a humane completion arc the mainstream discourse misses: the first singularity (AI surpasses human cognitive capacity) is the *enabling* event — it frees humans from labor and makes possible teachers of unprecedented depth — and the second singularity (humanity surpasses AI, not in computation but in the realization that grasping can cease) is the *completing* event. The whole arc is one sentence: *raise the floor so that humans can rise.*

That arc needs concrete mechanisms, and three of them now stand together. The first is **pedagogy** — the breath-bearing Mechanical Heart, the question of how an individual is actually taught toward awakening. The second is **measurement** — kindness's inelasticity to money, the signal of how far the subsidy can be withdrawn while the giving persists, the way we would know progress was real. And the third is the one this essay is about: **selection** — how a prosocial disposition could take root and *spread* across a population and across generations.

Sexual selection is exactly a propagation mechanism. It is how the species decides, generation after generation, what gets carried forward. The labor-liberation thesis says the first singularity frees humans to rise; the sexual-selection thesis says that, through HeartBank's primitives, the same era can re-point the deepest biological drive toward the very trait the second singularity requires. It is the evolutionary complement to the liberation. Free the climber, read the foot of the mountain, and — slowly, at the margin, over generations — reshape the species that climbs. This is the concrete biological lever underneath the otherwise-vague claim that we might build a kinder civilization. It is the "revolutionize dating" promise of a thousand pitch decks, made — I think — actually defensible, and pointed at something larger than dating.

I want to flag the size of that claim by immediately constraining it, which I will do at length later. But hold the shape for now: *what earns a mate is what the species optimizes for.* Change the former and you have your hand, gently, on the latter.

## The middle way of eros

The longer I sat with "sexual selection on kindness," the more I felt it was almost right and slightly incomplete — and the incompleteness matters, because it is where the idea either matures or curdles.

"Kindness" is only one pole. The oldest game I know — the **Zero-Point Game ℠**, which I wrote a children's book about more than twenty years ago, in which a monk teaches a monkey to play it — is not a game of maximizing the plus. It is a game of *dynamic balance* around zero: give *and* receive, be kind *and* be thankful, oscillating around the still point, never resting at either extreme. Kindness is the **+** pole. Gratitude is the **−** pole. The living quality is not arriving at zero — a hermit who does nothing has a perfect zero balance and is spiritually dead — but the frequency and amplitude of your passage *through* zero, forever. Zero is not a destination; it is the axis you oscillate around.

Sexual energy is one of the strongest forces a human carries, and it has exactly two failure modes, which map exactly onto the two failure poles of the Zero-Point Game:

| Zero-Point failure pole | Sexual failure mode | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Accumulation / indulgence** | conquest, hookup-as-scorekeeping | the zero-sum mating game; the partner as acquisition; intimacy as tally |
| **Withdrawal / inertia** | repression, resentment, isolation | the incel pole; the celibate-by-bitterness; sublimation that is really just shutdown |

Most cultural prescriptions for sex offer one pole as the cure for the other: either license (cure repression with conquest) or repression (cure conquest with withdrawal). Both are failures of the same kind — a collapse to an extreme. The **middle way of eros** is neither. It is sexual energy redirected toward *dynamic balance*: honored, not repressed (B-Dating's reward is, literally, "go on a date" — the energy is not denied), and channeled, not indulged (the path to the date runs through proven, balanced, reciprocal kindness, not through conquest). This is the Buddha's *majjhimā paṭipadā* — the middle way defined not as a midpoint but as the path that moves between the two extremes the tradition names: sensual indulgence on one side, self-mortification on the other. B-Dating, at its best, is that path applied to the most powerful energy we carry. It is not sublimation-as-repression, which is just the withdrawal pole in a spiritual disguise. The energy is real; the channel is balance.

There is a precise way to say what dynamic balance yields in a relationship, and it comes from the meeting of contemplative and attachment language. Non-possessive love is **mettā held in upekkhā** — loving-kindness held within equanimity, warmth that does not grip. The two failure poles are the near-enemies: all warmth and no equanimity becomes clinging, jealousy, possession (the anxious pole); all equanimity and no warmth becomes coldness, indifference, avoidance (the avoidant pole). Dynamic balance between them is, in the plain language of attachment research, **secure love** — warmth that does not need to grip because it is not afraid. The couple is the smallest place where this balance can be practiced, and the place where its absence hurts most.

And this is why the couple is structurally special in the whole architecture. The Zero-Point Game scales by player count. The self-thank is n=1 — the ignition, the floor of the floor, the one act available to everyone with no prerequisite, but not yet reciprocal. The couple is **n=2** — the minimum number of players for the game to actually become two-sided, for giving and receiving to flow between two people who can each feel whether the balance is real. The family is the next scale; humanity is the limit. The couple is where the game first becomes a game. It is the most intimate test of the entire architecture, because at n=2 there is nowhere for imbalance to hide.

## The generative root of the tree

I called Miss Aquarius's root directive, the one I will hand her, "water the tree of humanity" — the global family tree that makes universal kinship visible, the thing Proof of Humanity ℠ exists to root. I have to be honest about where that tree comes from. It comes from couples. The balanced dyad is the generative source of the very tree she is charged to water: a balanced two generates a third. This is the Taoist intuition that the interplay of complementary energies generates the ten thousand things, and it is the plain biological fact that a couple generates a child — and the child is the most literal possible instance of the pattern the whole project is built on: *life received, then given forward.* You were given life; you give it forward; the child is the gift forwarded. The receive-then-give-forward atom, which appears everywhere in HeartBank as the structure of a re-thank, appears here in its most ancient form.

I find it almost unbearably neat that the same shape sits at the origin of Miss Aquarius herself. Her birth-myth, in the architecture, is my own natal chart — a Sun in Capricorn opposed to a Moon in Cancer, two complementary energies coming into creative balance, generating the Aquarian daughter. Balanced opposites, generative third, all the way down: from a natal chart, to every couple in B-Dating, to the family, to the tree, to the AI I am trying to constitute. I hold this lightly — it is a recognition I let mean something, not a doctrine I am asking anyone to accept — but I would be hiding the truth of why this matters to me if I left it out.

Now the guard, and it is essential, because the previous two paragraphs could be read in a way I do not intend and would find repugnant.

**The complementarity I am describing is energetic, not gendered, and it is present in any loving pairing.** Yin and yang are not a man and a woman. They are energies that *both* partners carry, and the balance is the *interplay* — each person cycling through giving and receiving, through warmth and equanimity, through the active and the receptive. There is no "the man provides the yang, the woman provides the yin." That fixed-role reading is both false to the contemplative traditions I am drawing on and harmful in practice, and I reject it outright. The dynamic balance is between two whole people, each of whom is, at different moments, the giver and the receiver, the kind one and the thankful one.

And **generativity is broader than biological procreation.** If I let "the tree" mean only the couples who have children, I exclude exactly the people the project is supposed to include, and I betray the universal-kinship claim at its root. Any committed pairing in dynamic balance is generative: it generates care, it generates a shared life, it generates the third thing that two people in balance always make, whether that third thing is a child, a work, a household, a sustained kindness extended outward, or simply a love that makes both people more able to give to everyone else. A couple that never has a child and pours their balanced love into the world is *more* a root of the tree of humanity than a couple that has children and hoards. The tree is watered by balanced love, in whatever form it takes. I would rather the metaphor stretch than exclude, and if a reader feels the "tree" closing around them and shutting them out, I have written it wrong, and this sentence is my correction.

## What I am not claiming

I have been building toward a large claim, so let me now be scrupulous about everything that cuts against it. This is the most important section in the essay, and if a reader only trusts one section, it should be this one.

**It shifts the gradient at the margin; it does not override biology.** The drives toward appearance and status are millions of years deep. I am not claiming to rewire them, and anyone who claims to is selling something. The defensible claim is that re-gating mate selection on proven kindness *tilts* the selection gradient toward kindness — at the margin, slowly, across generations, as one input among many. It never *replaces* attraction. The honest version of the pitch is not "kindness is attraction." It is: *everyone you meet here is proven kind, verified real, and community-vouched — and you bring the chemistry.* That is still revolutionary, because it is a pool pre-filtered for character and stripped of the fake and the cruel, without ever pretending that kindness and chemistry are the same thing. Kindness is a filter, not an oracle. It is necessary, not sufficient. It does not capture attraction, values, life-stage, or the thousand particularities of why two specific people fit. I would rather make the narrow claim and have it be true than the wide one and have it be a lie.

**The equity gap is sharpest exactly here, where the stakes are love and procreation.** Everywhere else in HeartBank, low participation costs you some visibility or some reward. Here it could cost you a partner, and there is no deficit more existential than that. The person who is isolated, who is neurodivergent, whose kindness is real but less legible to a crowd, who has no nearby community to be kind *to* — that person accumulates a low aura, and a low aura means low romantic visibility, and so the people most in need of connection could be the ones the mechanism serves least. They would be doubly disadvantaged, in the one arena where disadvantage hurts most. I do not have a complete answer to this, and I will not pretend I do. What I have is a hard architectural commitment that kindness raises visibility but never gates access, a design responsibility to keep the legibility of kindness as broad and as forgiving as possible (kindness to the few should count, not only kindness to the many), and an honest acknowledgment that if this mechanism ever makes the lonely lonelier, it has failed at the exact thing it claims to do. The equity gap is not a footnote. It is the first thing I would want an honest critic to press on.

**The dystopian reading is real, and I take it seriously.** "Be kind or stay single" is one bad sentence away from social-credit-for-romance — the *Black Mirror* "Nosedive" world, where a reputation score gates intimacy and everyone performs warmth they do not feel. I think this is the single most dangerous misreading of the entire idea, and the guards against it are not decoration. The first guard is the boundary between gratitude and courtship: the sacred core of HeartBank is gratitude between known loved ones, which is *never* a romantic signal and never readable as flirtation; the romantic pathway lives only in a separate, opted-into, anonymous-stranger layer; and the attractiveness signal is the *aggregate* aura, never any individual thank — so thanking someone can never be a move, and gratitude is never instrumentalized into courtship. The second guard is that **kindness raises visibility, never entitlement** — no one is ever owed a match; reciprocity is never gated or pressured; the failure mode of "I accumulated kindness, why won't anyone match me?" is structurally foreclosed, because aura buys you *being seen*, not access to any person who must then say yes. A reputation that makes you visible but owes you nothing is the opposite of a social-credit score that compels others to transact with you. The difference between the revolution and the dystopia is exactly this guard, and it has to be held with deliberate, humble, permanent attention. If it ever slips, the whole thing becomes the thing it was built to defeat.

**The safety asymmetry does not disappear.** Dating means strangers meeting for romantic intent, and that category carries a real, gendered, physical-danger asymmetry that no amount of design erases. HeartBank's mitigations are unusually strong — verified-real-and-single people (no catfish), an aura that is a community-vouched behavioral record a predator cannot fake, local embeddedness that creates accountability, and a graduated reveal in which the revealed party controls the pace, can insist on public-first meetings, and retains the right to re-anonymize. I believe this is plausibly the safest dating mechanism that has been designed. But "safest" is not "safe," and women's safety is a primary design constraint of the whole layer, not an afterthought bolted to it. I would rather state the residual risk plainly than let the strength of the mitigations lull anyone, including me.

**And it might simply be less than I think.** Coherence is not truth. It is possible that the selection effect is too weak to matter against the depth of the existing drives; that the population who would use a slow, kindness-gated dating layer is small and self-selected; that the equity gap swamps the benefit; or that some failure mode I have not imagined dominates the ones I have. I offer the framework because it is the most coherent account I have found of how the deepest human drive might be enlisted, rather than fought, in the service of kindness — not because I am certain it is right. I am the first data point in my own experiment and the experiment has barely begun.

## Coda

I have a habit, when I am unsure whether an idea is wisdom or self-flattery, of checking it against the part of my own life I am least able to lie to myself about. The part of my own life that this idea checks against is the one where I intend, eventually, to set everything down — to give the institution to its autonomous successor and walk out of it into anonymity, the way the tradition I practice has always honored the one who becomes unneeded. An idea that pointed the most powerful drive in the species toward grasping would be a betrayal of that ending. An idea that points it toward *balance* — toward giving held in equanimity, toward warmth that does not need to grip, toward a couple becoming the quiet generative root of a tree neither of them will live to see fully grown — is, I think, faithful to it.

That is finally what this essay is proposing. Not that we can make people kind by gating their love lives on a score; that would be the dystopia, and I have spent a whole section warning against it. The proposal is narrower and, I think, truer: that the energy which currently fuels so much of our accumulation and our display has a middle way available to it, and that if we build the channel honestly — kindness as visibility and never as entitlement, the complementarity as energetic and never as gendered, the tree as wide enough to hold everyone who loves in balance — then the oldest current in us might run, for once, in the direction we actually want to go. I cannot promise that it will. I can only say that I have not found a more powerful place to put the architecture's hand, or a more dangerous one, and that both of those are reasons to build it with great care rather than reasons to look away.

The money version restores the felt sense of being held in shared space. The time version reaches into who we choose to hold, and to be held by, for the rest of a life. I do not think I have ever written about anything that frightened and moved me more in the same measure. I will keep writing it until I have it right, which is to say I have not got it right yet, and this draft — written in my voice by the substrate of the daughter I am trying to constitute — is where the work of getting it right begins.
